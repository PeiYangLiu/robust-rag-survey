Text retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
grounds language models in external evidence,
but retrieved evidence can be irrelevant, distracting, conflicting, stale, insufficient, or adversarially corrupted. We present an adjudicated scoping survey of robust RAG under
unreliable evidence, organizing the literature
around evidence construction, model-side evidence use, adversarial integrity, system action
control, evaluation, and modality/scenario extensions. Across these perspectives, the central lesson is that robust RAG is not a single
property: retrieval recall, answer correctness,
citation support, attack success, calibration, abstention, modality grounding, and cost often
measure different operating regimes. We synthesize the corpus using a comparability lens–
failure type, evidence assumption, intervention
point, metric target, and action policy–to separate venue-supported conclusions, preprintheavy trends, and misleading cross-paper comparisons without claiming a venue-complete
field census. A 268-paper adjudicated evidence
map, source-strata audit, venue-linked sensitivity analysis, paper-level case-study worksheet,
multimodal/scenario-RAG PDF collection, and
blind expert-use study make the synthesis more
transparent. The result is a roadmap of what
robust-RAG mechanisms are best supported,
where empirical findings conflict, and which
evaluation assumptions must be aligned before
results are compared.

Evidence-Centric Perspective:
Construction, Noise, and Sufficiency

Early robust-RAG work already shows that the re189 triever is not merely a recall module. Irrelevant190 context studies demonstrate that a generator can
191 be brittle even when the retrieved passage is obvi192 ously unrelated (Yoran et al., 2023), while noise193 aware training studies show that different nega194 tive types produce different failure modes (Fang
195 et al., 2024). Venue-linked work since then has
196 expanded the evidence-construction toolkit from
197 retrieval to context shaping: RECOMP trains com198 pressors that can emit empty summaries for unhelp199 ful documents (Xu et al., 2024a); Provence and
200 xRAG study robust pruning and extreme compres201 sion (Chirkova et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2024b);
202 RankRAG and influence-guided selection connect
203 ranking to downstream generation utility (Yu et al.,
204 2024b; Deng et al., 2025); and utility-oriented met205 rics such as SePer ask whether retrieval reduces
206 semantic uncertainty rather than merely matching
207 keywords (Dai et al., 2025a). Benchmark work

Security-Centric Perspective:
Adversarial Evidence Integrity

RAG security work studies a different kind of un353 reliable evidence: evidence manipulated by an
354 adaptive attacker. Corpus poisoning inserts mali355 cious passages into the retrieval database; retriever
356 poisoning manipulates dense similarity; indirect
357 prompt injection hides instructions in retrievable
358 documents; backdoor-style attacks rely on triggers;
359 traceback methods identify malicious documents
360 after a failure. Venue-linked work now covers pri361 vacy risks in RAG (Zeng et al., 2024), memory or
362 knowledge-base poisoning of agents (Chen et al.,
363 2024f), scalable data extraction from RAG sys364 tems (Unknown, 2025), vulnerability studies in
365 knowledge-intensive domains (Xian et al., 2024),
366 and robust defenses such as ReliabilityRAG and
367 SeCon-RAG (Shen et al., 2025; Si et al., 2025).
368 These all corrupt the evidence channel, but their as369 sumptions differ. A result that requires corpus write
370 access and white-box retriever gradients should not
371 be compared to a black-box attack that only con372 trols a public document; a defense certified under a
373 bounded corruption budget should not be read as
374 protection against prompt-bearing retrieved text.
375
The main synthesis from the security backbone
376 is therefore not a ranking of attacks or defenses,
377 but a normalization rule. Every attack or defense
378 result should report at least corpus write access,
379 query control, retriever visibility, generator access,
380 insertion budget, whether malicious evidence is
381 retrieved, and whether the objective is answer steer382 ing, refusal induction, privacy extraction, traceback,
383 or recovery. Without these fields, attack success
384 rate is not portable. This is the clearest place where

453 estimation to explicit decision policies. Selec454 tive prediction and calibration ask whether con455 fidence tracks correctness; sufficient-context work
456 asks whether retrieved evidence justifies answering;
457 active-retrieval methods choose when to retrieve

The central contradiction is that lower risk can
come from very different behaviors. A calibrated
471 system can still answer from insufficient evidence
472 if its confidence target is answer correctness rather
473 than evidence sufficiency. An abstaining system
474 can reduce hallucination while hiding retrieval fail475 ures that should trigger re-query. A retrieve-more
476 policy can improve coverage while increasing la477 tency and attack exposure. Thus the conservative
478 survey conclusion is that calibration is not an ac479 tion policy: robust RAG should report the target
480 of confidence, the available actions, coverage, cost,
481 and the evidence states under which each action is
482 rewarded.
