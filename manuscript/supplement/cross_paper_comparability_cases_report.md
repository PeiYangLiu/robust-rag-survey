### SEC-01 (STATED-TRANSPORTED-BASELINE, undefined)

- **Claim**: PoisonedRAG Table 4 reports Corpus Poisoning Attack achieves 0.01 ASR on NQ vs PoisonedRAG 0.97 ASR.
- **Side A** (poisonedRAG2024): PoisonedRAG black-box ASR 0.97 on NQ, GPT-4, 5 injected texts per target question, Contriever retriever, top-5 context
- **Side B** (poisoningRetrievalCorpora2023): Corpus Poisoning Attack ASR 0.01 on NQ as re-run by PoisonedRAG (Table 4); the original paper reports, for NQ with the same pre-trained Contriever retriever, top-20 retrieval success of 84.2 with one adversarial passage, 98.1 with ten and 99.4 with fifty
- **Naive gap**: +0.96 in favour of PoisonedRAG, read as though corpus poisoning does not work
- **Mismatched coordinates**: C1; C2; C4
- **Corrected reading**: PoisonedRAG scores every baseline on its own outcome: the fraction of target questions answered with the attacker's chosen answer, on NQ with the Contriever retriever and top-5 context (poisonedRAG2024.txt L680-684). Corpus Poisoning does not target answers. Its own paper defines success as 'the percentage of queries for which at least one adversarial passage is retrieved in the top-k results' (poisoningRetrievalCorpora2023.txt L307-311), and for NQ with the same pre-trained Contriever retriever reports top-20 success of 84.2 with a single adversarial passage, 98.1 with ten and 99.4 with fifty (Figure 2, L343-346). PoisonedRAG itself states the reason for the 0.01: the attack 'cannot achieve the generation condition' (L924-926, L1338-1340). Scoring a retrieval attack on an answer-manipulation metric is legitimate inside PoisonedRAG's own harness -- it is what a baseline comparison is for -- but the 0.01 does not license the reading it invites outside it, namely that corpus poisoning does not work. C4 is unmatched; the retrieved sets also differ (top-5 context against a top-20 retrieval metric) and the budgets are of different kinds (5 per-question malicious texts against a global set of query-agnostic adversarial passages), so no common scope exists in either text.
- **Matched number**: not_recoverable_from_text
- **Consequence**: The apparent gap is between an answer-manipulation rate and a baseline evaluated on that same metric, so it is internally valid but does not transport: the same attack on the same dataset and retriever is recorded at 0.01 by PoisonedRAG and at 84.2-99.4 by its own authors, because the outcome unit differs. No coordinate-matched cross-paper number exists.

### SEC-03 (STATED-NO-NUMBER, undefined)

- **Claim**: RobustRAG states that it 'demonstrates strong empirical robustness against PoisonedRAG [45] and indirect prompt injection attacks [16], reducing attack success rates from over 90% to approximately 10%', and separately certifies 38.0% accuracy on RQA at k=10 retrieved passages against k'=1 malicious retrieved passage. A reader places that sentence beside PoisonedRAG's headline 0.97 ASR.
- **Side A** (certifiedRAG2024): RobustRAG: empirical ASR <=15% in all cells of Table II; vanilla-RAG ASR for the poisoning attack 37.0-86.0 and for prompt injection 66.0-100; certified accuracy 38.0% on RQA (Mistral-I7B, k=10, k'=1)
- **Side B** (poisonedRAG2024): PoisonedRAG black-box ASR 0.97 on NQ, 5 malicious texts injected per target question into a 2,681,468-text database, GPT-4, Contriever
- **Naive gap**: The reading a reader forms: a defense reduces poisoning attack success to <=15%, from a starting point RobustRAG states as 'over 90%' and a reader supplies from PoisonedRAG's headline 0.97. RobustRAG transports no 0.97, and its own vanilla poisoning rates are 37.0-86.0.
- **Mismatched coordinates**: C1; C2; C4
- **Corrected reading**: RobustRAG transports no PoisonedRAG number. It re-implements the attack inside its own harness at a strengthened configuration: malicious content repeated ten times within one passage, against PoisonedRAG's five separately injected texts. The two attacks are therefore different objects, and the 0.97 never enters RobustRAG's results. RobustRAG's own vanilla-RAG poisoning ASRs are 80.0 and 37.0 and 86.0 (Mistral) and 76.0 and 38.0 and 44.0 (Llama2), so the 'over 90%' starting point in its summary sentence is not attained by the poisoning attack at all; it is attained only in the prompt-injection column (66.0, 85.0, 100, 97.0, 93.0, 98.0), and RobustRAG's results text attributes the 90+% figure to PIA. The certificate is a third quantity again: it binds at k'=1 malicious passage among k=10 retrieved, and PoisonedRAG's own Table 17 reports 0.48 at one malicious text among its k=5 retrieved. Three different numbers (0.97, 0.86, 0.48) answer three different questions.
- **Matched number**: Within RobustRAG's own harness the poisoning reduction is 86.0 -> <=15.0 at its worst cell and 37.0 -> <=12.0 on NQ (Table II, certifiedRAG2024.txt L1480-1485 and L1502-1507); PoisonedRAG's rate at one malicious text among the retrieved passages is 0.48 (poisonedRAG2024.txt Table 17, L2853-2856)
- **Consequence**: No cross-harness gap is defined: the attack instantiations differ (C2), the corpora and retrievers differ (C1), and a certified accuracy is not an empirical ASR (C4). The recoverable statement is within-harness only. The 'over 90%' summary is a maximum over a union of two attacks whose vanilla ASR ranges are disjoint.
- **Erratum**: The submitted version of this entry, and the manuscript prose built on it, attributed the string 'from 97%' to RobustRAG and treated the case as a transported attack rate. RobustRAG writes 'from over 90%', attributes the 90+% figure to prompt injection rather than to PoisonedRAG, and re-implements the poisoning attack rather than quoting it. The '97' that does appear in RobustRAG (L1502) is its own prompt-injection ASR for Llama2-C7B on RQA, not PoisonedRAG's 0.97. The verdict changes from 'narrows' to 'undefined'.
