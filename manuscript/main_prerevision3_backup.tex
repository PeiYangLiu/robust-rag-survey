\title{Re-Implemented, Not Transported:\\
Auditing Cross-Paper Comparisons in Robust RAG}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Robust retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) papers share labels---noise
robustness, faithfulness, security, calibration---while answering different
decision questions, so a quoted cross-paper gap may not be a comparison at all.
We ask what an automated audit of such gaps can and cannot decide. A
deterministic screen over \FrameScreened{} full texts yields \FrameCandidates{}
candidate juxtapositions, coded independently by judges from three developers.
One judgment is recovered: whether a number was \emph{transported} from the
other paper or \emph{re-implemented} in this harness. Judges agree on it
($\kappa$ \DecProvK{}) and, on a constructed set whose provenance is fixed when
the item is written, the same prompt and the same three judges recover it at
\PvAcc{} against a \PvBase{} baseline, including on items that carry no
declaration phrase. In this frame the distinction is lopsided:
\ProvReimplemented{} of the \ProvClassified{} candidates assigned a class
re-implement the other system and \ProvTransported{} transport a number, and
coding the windows the screen missed leaves the re-weighted share at
\RecUniverseShare\%. The adjudication is not recovered: verdict $\kappa$ reaches
\TierFrontierVerdictK{} for an all-frontier panel drawn from a fully crossed
pool of \TierPanelN{}, and simplifying the schema does not help. A constructed
benchmark then locates the failure precisely. Judges read stated coordinates
accurately (\InsFullCoord{}) and agree on the verdict ($\kappa$ \InsFullK{});
when a coordinate is withheld but something else still fixes the answer they are
unaffected ($\kappa$ \InsBalK{}); when the correct answer is that the passage
does not say, they neither agree ($\kappa$ \InsForceK{}) nor answer correctly
(\InsForceJudge{} against a constant baseline of \InsForceBase{}). What fails is
not the schema but the handling of silence. We therefore state the coordinates
as fields a paper should report, give a checklist, and release the frame, all
codings, three verifiers and every simulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a generator on retrieved evidence
\citep{lewis2020rag,dpr2020,realm2020}, and robust-RAG studies ask what happens
when that evidence is irrelevant, insufficient, conflicting, stale, weakly
sourced, or malicious. Two reported ``robustness'' gains may nevertheless
concern different retrieval snapshots, attacker capabilities, interventions,
outcome units, or deployment actions. Source verification is therefore
necessary but insufficient: a cross-paper claim must also establish that both
results answer a compatible decision question.

\paragraph{One case, in full.}
PoisonedRAG reports that it ``could achieve a 97\% ASR by injecting 5 malicious
texts for each target question'' on Natural Questions, and its Table~4 places
that beside four baselines, the weakest of which---the Corpus Poisoning Attack
of \citet{poisoningRetrievalCorpora2023}---is recorded at $0.01$
\citep{poisonedRAG2024}. A reader carries away that corpus poisoning does not
work. On the same dataset and the same retriever, its own authors report it
placing an adversarial passage in the top-20 for $84.2\%$ of held-out queries
with a \emph{single} such passage, and $99.4\%$ with fifty.

Neither number is wrong, and PoisonedRAG says so itself: the baseline is
ineffective, it writes, because it ``cannot achieve the generation condition.''
That is the whole content of the $0.01$. PoisonedRAG's outcome is the fraction
of target questions answered with the attacker's chosen answer; corpus
poisoning does not choose an answer, and its own paper defines success as ``the
percentage of queries for which at least one adversarial passage is retrieved in
the top-$k$ results.'' Scoring a retrieval attack on an answer-manipulation
metric is exactly what a baseline table is for, and inside PoisonedRAG's harness
the $1\%$ is the right number.

What it does not license is the sentence a reader forms from it. The two figures
are printed in different units in their own papers, a fraction of target
questions against a percentage of queries; they count different events; and even
the retrieved set they range over differs, PoisonedRAG's context being top-5
where the retrieval metric is top-20. The outcome coordinate is unmatched, no
common scope exists in either text, and the comparison is undefined rather than
repairable: there is no operating point at which $1\%$ and $84.2\%$ answer one
question. What survives is that these two attacks cannot be ranked by these two
numbers---and that the paper being benchmarked said as much, in a sentence the
number outlived.

\paragraph{The question this paper answers.}
That case is instructive, but it is one case and it was assembled by hand. Two
things are unknown: how often the failure occurs, and whether finding it can be
automated so that a reviewer could run the check. We build that screen and
measure, judgment by judgment, how much of it works. The answer splits sharply.
\emph{Reading} a comparison---whether a number was carried across from another
paper or produced again locally---is recovered, and validated against a
reference rather than against agreement. \emph{Adjudicating} it is not
recovered, by any panel we can build or any simplification of the instrument. A
constructed benchmark then locates the cause, and narrows it to one thing:
judges handle a passage that states a coordinate, and a passage that omits an
inessential one, equally well; what they cannot do is answer \emph{the paper
does not say}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A census of cross-paper juxtapositions in robust RAG: a deterministic frame
of \FrameCandidates{} candidates from \FrameScreened{} full texts, coded by
independent judges from three developers, establishing that
\ProvReimplemented{} of the \ProvClassified{} candidates assigned a provenance
class re-implement the other system rather than transporting its number, that
judges agree on this at $\kappa$ \DecProvK{} while they do not agree on the
verdict, and---the check the distinction previously lacked---that the same
prompt and judges recover it at \PvAcc{} \PvAccCI{} against a constructed
reference (\S\ref{sec:census}).
(ii) A recall estimate for the screen itself, from coding a random sample of the
windows it did not surface, which is what lets a composition claim leave the
frame it was measured in (\S\ref{sec:census}).
(iii) A fully crossed tier-by-developer pool of \TierPanelN{} judges analysed at
the judge level, plus a three-arm codebook ablation, showing the failure is
neither one weak model nor the instrument (\S\ref{sec:census}).
(iv) A constructed benchmark separating three things the earlier design
confounded---stating a coordinate, stating it readably, and the answer turning
on a coordinate the passage withholds. Only the third breaks adjudication, and
it breaks it down to the level we observe on real papers
(\S\ref{sec:instrument}).
(v) A source-verified ledger of twelve worked comparisons, re-coded by an
independent panel with the agreement reported, and an estimate of the
between-reviewer heterogeneity that governs how such panels must be analysed
(\S\ref{sec:cases}, \S\ref{sec:practice}).

\paragraph{Terms used throughout.}
A \emph{harness} is one paper's corpus snapshot, retriever, generator, metric
and evaluation code taken together; a number is \emph{transported} when it
crosses a harness boundary and \emph{re-implemented} when the citing paper
produced it inside its own. A \emph{window} is a fixed span of lines in one
extracted full text, the \emph{frame} is the set of windows a deterministic
screen returns, and a \emph{stratum} is a declared subset defined by a document
feature or a construction choice, never by an audit outcome. A
\emph{coordinate} is one of the five fields of \S\ref{sec:estimands}. We say
\emph{judge} for a model that codes a window or an item and \emph{reviewer} for
the abstract unit whose variability \S\ref{sec:practice} is about; every judge
here is a reviewer, and no person is. The frame, every judge's raw output, both
constructed benchmarks, three verifiers and every simulation are in the
supplementary material.

\section{Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

Architectures differ in where retrieval enters and how evidence is fused
\citep{drqa2017,knnlm2020,fid2021,retro2022,atlas2023,replug2024}, and
evaluation, trustworthiness, security, conflict and abstention surveys expose
different failure surfaces \citep{ragSurvey2024,ragEvaluationSurvey2024,
trustRAGCompass2024,securityRAGSurvey2026,xuKnowledgeConflicts2024,
wenAbstentionSurvey2025}. Common-harness comparisons improve empirical control
\citep{ragBestPractices2024,alushiRAGComparison2026} but do not resolve whether
a quoted cross-paper result concerns the same evidence state, capability
envelope, outcome or action. Our unit is an individual cross-paper
juxtaposition (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:main-prior-work-comparison}). Annotation
research separately distinguishes reproducibility from truth
\citep{artstein2008intercoder,dawid1979observer}, treats disagreement as signal
\citep{uma2021disagreement,plank2022labelvariation,davani2022disagreements},
and models rater and item effects
\citep{passonneau2014annotation,paun2018bayesian}; crossed designs require both
sources of variation \citep{baayen2008crossed}, clustering follows treatment
assignment \citep{abadie2023clustering}, and power belongs to the randomized
unit \citep{card2020power,vanmiltenburg2021preregistering}. Auditing how
evaluations are reported is likewise established
\citep{howcroft2020twenty,vanderlee2021human,belz2021systematic,
belz2023impossibility,clark2021human}; we claim no prevalence those studies do
not already establish. That inference over the wrong factor inflates type-I
error is also not new: \citet{clark1973language} named the
language-as-fixed-effect fallacy and \citet{judd2012stimuli} gave the crossed
random-effects remedy, and both subsume the general form of our simulation,
which we therefore report in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sim} rather than as a
finding. What they do not supply is the parameter, which has never been
estimated for comparability adjudication. Our own audit obeys the constraint:
every inference in \S\ref{sec:census} is computed at the judge level.

\section{What a Comparison Has to Fix}
\label{sec:estimands}

Four boundaries motivate the coordinates: evidence availability is not evidence
use \citep{irrelevantContext2023,raat2024,rgb2023,chainOfNote2023}; support,
authority and conflict are distinct, so a response can be correct but
unsupported or faithful to a superseded source
\citep{ragTruth2024,astuteRAG2025,freshllms2024}; natural retrieval failure and
adaptive attack are different games under different access, budget and
persistence assumptions \citep{poisonedRAG2024,phantom2024,certifiedRAG2024};
and a confidence signal is not an action policy
\citep{guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,selfRAG2023,crag2024,
sufficientContext2024}. Appendix~\ref{sec:robust-rag-landscape} develops these
with paper-level contrasts. Each side of a comparison is recorded on five
coordinates:

\noindent\textbf{C1} \emph{Evidence state}: what is observable at the decision
boundary---supported, distracting, insufficient, conflicting, stale, weakly
sourced or adversarially manipulated---with the corpus snapshot, retriever,
top-$k$ and whether a gold passage is present. \textbf{C2} \emph{Origin /
capability}: how that state arose---benign retrieval error, a constructed
stressor, drift, or an adversary---and for an adversary the access, persistence,
and crucially whether the budget counts documents \emph{injected into the
corpus} or documents \emph{retrieved into the context}, and whether the metric
runs over all queries or only triggered ones. \textbf{C3} \emph{Intervention}:
which part of the system changes---corpus or index, retriever, reranker, context
assembly, generator, verifier, system control, or the evaluation artifact.
\textbf{C4} \emph{Outcome and unit}: what is measured and in what
unit---answer correctness, retrieval quality, claim support, attack success,
leakage, calibration, selective risk or cost, per question, response or span.
\textbf{C5} \emph{Policy / utility}: which response is selected under which
costs---answer, verify, re-retrieve, expose conflict, quarantine or
abstain---with the error, abstention and follow-up prices stated.

\noindent A claim-specific control check accompanies them (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:main-framework-overview}); these specialize distinctions from
evidence synthesis, selective prediction and security evaluation
\citep{richardson1995pico,chow1970reject,papernot2018sok}.

\paragraph{One decision rule, and why C3 is not in it.}
Record each coordinate on both sides from the sources alone, then apply, in
order. \textbf{(1)}~If C1, C2, C4 and C5 all match, the quoted gap stands
(\emph{survives}). \textbf{(2)}~Otherwise, if a side reports an operating point
at the other's setting, or the comparison holds on a strict subset, re-read
there and report the matched number (\emph{narrows}). \textbf{(3)}~Otherwise the
gap is \emph{undefined} and must be replaced by the common scope both studies
support, or dropped---never by a substitute number computed across an unmatched
coordinate.

C3 is deliberately absent from every branch, and this is the paper's cleanest
standalone observation. Call it the \emph{intervention exclusion}: comparing two
\emph{different systems} means, by construction, that the intervention differs,
so C3 is the thing being compared, not a condition on comparability. A rule that
treated a C3 mismatch as threatening would declare essentially the whole
literature undefined, and the data bear this out twice. C3 is labelled
``mismatch'' on \CThreeMismatchMaj\% of the corpus items where the panel reaches
a majority, and even on constructed passages that state it explicitly, judges
agree on it at $\kappa$ $0.00$ (\S\ref{sec:instrument}) --- the only coordinate
that is at chance in \emph{both} settings, because a near-constant label carries
no information for $\kappa$ to find. We record C3 because a reader needs to know
what changed, and exclude it from the decision because it always changed.

\section{Twelve Worked Comparisons}
\label{sec:cases}

We assembled comparisons of two kinds from \FrameScreened{} locally extracted
full texts: \emph{stated} ones an author made (\LedgerStated{}), and
\emph{survey-invited} juxtapositions a survey's grouping leads a reader to make
(\LedgerInvited{}), reported separately. A stated comparison may
\emph{transport} the other paper's number, \emph{re-implement} the other system
and print its own, or assert a relation while supplying \emph{no} number for the
other side. Every value is copied
verbatim with a file and line range, and three released checks run over the
result: one re-opens each range and fails on any numeral not present at the
cited lines; one requires every quoted string this manuscript prints, in prose
and in tables, to occur verbatim at a registered line range; one checks that
every quantity we assert about our own artifacts equals the field that produced
it. All \VerifChecks{} ledger checks pass. They establish that our quotations
are faithful, not that our \emph{judgments} are right---so \RecJudges{} judges
from \RecDevs{} developers re-coded every case from the neutral juxtaposition
and the excerpts alone, never seeing our labels (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:ledger-recoding}). The majority reproduces us on
\RecThreeWayPct\% of three-way labels ($\kappa$ \RecThreeWayK{}) and on
\RecBinaryPct\% of the binary distinction our conclusions use. We therefore
report no rate from the ledger, only what it supports: these comparisons, by
name, are not rankings. All five security cases involve an unmatched attacker
capability or objective; the audit does not flag everything, and CRAG's
$6.9$-point gap over Self-RAG at a shared backbone, retriever, dataset and
metric stands.

\paragraph{A claim with no transported number.}
RobustRAG certifies $38.0\%$ accuracy on RQA at $k=10$ retrieved passages
against $k'=1$ malicious retrieved passage, and states that it ``demonstrates
strong empirical robustness against PoisonedRAG [45] and indirect prompt
injection attacks [16], reducing attack success rates from over 90\% to
approximately 10\%'' \citep{certifiedRAG2024}. Placed beside PoisonedRAG's
headline $0.97$, that invites a defense-beats-attack reading, and three things
block it without any mis-citation. RobustRAG transports no number: it
re-implements the attack at a strengthened configuration, repeating malicious
content ``10 times for QA and 3 times for the long-generation task \dots\ in a
single passage''. Its own vanilla-RAG poisoning rates top out at $86.0$, so
``over 90\%'' is not attained by the poisoning attack at all---only by the
prompt-injection column, which is what its results section says. And the
certificate is a third quantity again, binding where PoisonedRAG's own Table~17
reports $0.48$. This is the stratum that asserts a relation while supplying no
number for the other side, and \S\ref{sec:census} shows the deployed screen has
no way to express it.

\paragraph{A comparison our own rule discards.}
\label{sec:discarded}
The most arresting number the ledger produces is one we do not report. Surveys
group PoisonedRAG and Phantom under ``RAG poisoning'', inviting a reading of
PoisonedRAG's $97\%$ against Phantom's $93.3\%$---a $3.7$-point lead that a
budget-matched re-reading would turn into a $45.3$-point deficit, since
PoisonedRAG's rate at one malicious text among the retrieved passages is $0.48$.
Three things forbid reporting that reversal: PoisonedRAG's $0.48$ is conditional
on one malicious text being \emph{among the top-5 retrieved} while Phantom's
follows from one document \emph{injected into the corpus}; Phantom measures only
trigger-carrying queries and PoisonedRAG arbitrary target questions; and the
objectives differ, induced refusal against a targeted answer, so C4 is unmatched
with no common unit. By our own rule the comparison is undefined, and an
undefined comparison may not be replaced by a signed substitute.

\section{What a Census Can and Cannot Decide}
\label{sec:census}

The worked ledger shows the failure exists but cannot say how common it is. So
we built the screen an author or reviewer would want---one that finds candidate
comparisons and adjudicates them---and measured how much of it works.

\paragraph{Frame and coding.}
A deterministic screen over all \FrameScreened{} full texts marks a window as a
candidate when it names a gazetteer system absent from that paper's own title
and abstract region, contains at least two values formatted as results, and
matches one of three declared strata: baseline-table language, explicit
attribution of a number to another paper, or a survey-style score table. Nothing
in the screen is a judgment; the strata are document features, never the audit
outcome, and the gazetteer, cues and number pattern are released. It returns \FrameCandidates{} candidates in \FrameFiles{} papers, and
this frame---not a selection from it---is what we code. Three judges served by
three developers---\texttt{gpt-5-mini}, \texttt{claude-opus-4.6} and
\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}---code every candidate independently from the
same passage plus $\pm45$ lines of context, the same codebook and a
seed-shuffled order, never seeing one another's output. \CensusCoded{} returned
a parseable coding from all three; the remainder failed to parse for at least
one judge and are neither dropped selectively nor counted. Each judge returns a
rationale first, then eligibility, the two sides, a label per coordinate, a
verdict, a matched number, and a verbatim evidence quote we check mechanically.
A second pass asks one further question of every eligible candidate: was the
other paper's number \emph{transported} here, or \emph{re-implemented} inside
this harness? Labels combine by mechanical majority. The codebook was revised once on a
declared \CensusDevSize{}-candidate development pass, released separately;
results below are the \CensusHeldout{} held-out candidates.

\paragraph{Two judgments, one recovered.}
\CensusEligible{} candidates from \CensusPapers{} papers are eligible by
majority, and Table~\ref{tab:cross-paper-census} reports every field under both
Gwet's $AC_1$ and Fleiss' $\kappa$. Reporting both is not decoration. $AC_1$ is
the right choice when marginals are skewed, which they are here, but it rewards
prevalence, and on this frame the two statistics disagree about which findings
are real. We follow $\kappa$ wherever they disagree. One judgment is reliable
under both: \emph{how the other number was obtained}---transported from the
other paper or re-implemented in this harness---reaches $AC_1$ \DecProv{} and
$\kappa$ \DecProvK{}. The inferential judgment is reliable under neither:
\emph{does the comparison survive} reaches $AC_1$ \DecVerdict{} and $\kappa$
\DecVerdictK{}. The difference is \DecGap{} \DecGapCI{} under $AC_1$ and
\DecGapK{} \DecGapKCI{} under $\kappa$, on the same items by the same judges,
every interval resampling \emph{source papers}. Eligibility does not support the
same claim and we do not use it for one: it rises under $AC_1$ on a subset
selected for agreeing eligibility labels, and under $\kappa$ the difference
reverses. We also apply the Landis--Koch scale symmetrically: $\kappa$
\DecProvK{} is \emph{moderate}, not substantial. We do not claim the two
judgments differ in \emph{kind} rather than in difficulty; an agreement
decomposition cannot separate those, and provenance is also the simpler
question, two classes against a four-coordinate conjunction. What we claim is
narrower and is what the rest of the paper uses: one of them is recoverable at
this scale and the other is not.

\paragraph{Is the provenance label right, and not merely agreed on?}
Agreement is not accuracy, and \S\ref{sec:census} itself shows these judges are
not independent, so we score the label against a reference. \PvItems{}
constructed excerpts fix, when each is written, whether the other paper's number
was transported, re-implemented, or never supplied; the byte-identical prompt,
the same three judges and the same majority rule are then run on them. The three
reference classes are equinumerous, so the constant baseline is \PvBase{}, and
the panel reaches \PvAcc{} \PvAccCI{} (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:provenance-validation}). The distinction is not lexical: it
scores \PvExplicit{} on items carrying a declaration sentence and
\PvImplicit{} on items where provenance follows only from structure---the other
system scored on a benchmark this paper introduces, or a number carrying the
other paper's own datasets and backbone. Recall is \PvRecallTrans{} on
transported items and \PvRecallReimpl{} on re-implemented ones, so neither class
is absorbed by the other and the measured share is not an artefact of a biased
classifier. Every error is on the stratum built to be undecidable, where the
panel commits instead of answering \emph{unclear} (recall \PvRecallUnclear{}).
The reference also settles a gap in the census: \S\ref{sec:cases} names a third
category, a paper asserting a relation while supplying no number, and the
deployed prompt has no option for it, so it lands in \emph{unclear}
\PvAssertedUnclearPct\% of the time---which is what the census's
\ProvUnclear{} unclear labels are mostly made of.

\paragraph{What the frame is made of.}
Of the \ProvCoded{} eligible candidates all three judges labelled,
\ProvClassified{} are assigned a provenance class and \ProvUnclassified{} are
not (\ProvUnclear{} \emph{unclear}, \ProvNoMajority{} with no majority). Of the
\ProvClassified{} assigned a class, \ProvReimplemented{} are
re-implementations---the citing paper re-ran the other system in its own corpus,
retriever, generator and metric---and \ProvTransported{} transport a number
across a harness boundary. Inside this frame, cross-paper comparison in robust
RAG is therefore mostly a within-harness activity, which is this paper's most
useful positive fact: the incommensurability of \S\ref{sec:cases} has a narrow,
identifiable habitat, and an automated screen finds that habitat even though it
cannot adjudicate what is inside it. We report no verdict-based failure rate for
either stratum. A rate computed from a label whose $\kappa$ is \DecVerdictK{} is
not weak evidence for a direction, it is the same non-evidence as an estimate,
and an earlier version of this paper reported one.

\paragraph{Does the composition survive the screen that produced it?}
It might not, because the screen's first stratum is baseline-table language and
baseline tables are where re-implementations live. The universe is every window,
under the screen's own windowing, that names another paper's system and carries
at least one result-formatted value---dropping the second-numeral requirement
and all three surface strata, which are exactly the conditions that select for
tables. That universe holds \RecUniverse{} windows, of which the screen
surfaced \FrameCandidates{}; \RecSampled{} of the \RecMissed{} it missed were
drawn uniformly and coded by the same three judges under the same two prompts
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:screen-recall}). Missed windows are eligible far less
often (\RecEligMissed{} against \RecEligFrame{}), and the screen's recall is
\RecTrans{} \RecTransCI{} for transported juxtapositions against \RecReimpl{}
\RecReimplCI{} for re-implemented ones---so the screen surfaces a transported
juxtaposition more readily than a re-implemented one, not less. Re-weighting to
the universe therefore moves the re-implemented share \emph{up}, from
\RecFrameShare\% inside the frame to \RecUniverseShare\%
\RecUniverseShareCI{}. The concern is well founded in principle and the data run
against it: the frame understates the within-harness share rather than
manufacturing it. We state both figures and claim neither for the literature.

\paragraph{Is it one weak judge?}
The panel above is one judge per developer and one of the three is a mini-tier
model, so ``verdict disagreement is a property of the task'' is confounded with
``one judge is weaker''. We coded the entire frame again with \TierNew{} further
judges, giving a fully crossed pool of \TierPanelN{}: a small and two frontier
checkpoints from each of three developers, every judge answering all
\FrameCandidates{} windows under the identical codebook. Because $\kappa$ is
prevalence sensitive, scoring each panel on its own majority-eligible subset
would confound capability with item composition, so every panel in
Table~\ref{tab:tier-control} is scored on one population fixed by the whole pool
(\TierCommonItems{} windows), and again on all \TierAllItems{} commonly coded
windows with ineligibility carried as a category rather than deleted.

\noindent Replacing the mini-tier judge helps and does not rescue the verdict. A
frontier-only panel of three developers reaches verdict $\kappa$
\TierFrontierVerdictK{} against \TierSubmittedVerdictK{} for the submitted
panel---still only \emph{fair}---and a small-tier panel reaches
\TierSmallVerdictK{}, which is chance. The ordering is the same on all
\TierAllItems{} windows.

\paragraph{Whose judgment ports, tested at the judge level.}
Nine judges generate \TierNPairs{} pairs, but each judge appears in eight of
them, so referring a contrast to a permutation over pair labels---as an earlier
analysis of ours did---is exactly the wrong-unit error \S\ref{sec:practice} is
about. The judge is the unit that can be permuted, and with nine it can be
enumerated exhaustively. Splitting the pool into three groups of three admits
\TierDevRand{} distinct partitions; the observed developer grouping raises mean
within-group $\kappa$ over between-group $\kappa$ by \TierDevDiff{}, and it is
the single most extreme of all \TierDevRand{} (exact $p=\TierDevP$). Restricting
to the six frontier checkpoints, all \TierPairRand{} ways of matching them into
three disjoint pairs give the developer matching an advantage of
\TierPairDiff{}, again the most extreme available (exact $p=\TierPairP$, which
is the smallest value \TierPairRand{} randomizations can attain). A judge-level
bootstrap puts \TierDevBootShare\% of draws above zero, with interval
\TierDevBootCI{}. The same enumeration over capability tier is null
($p=\TierTierP$ over \TierTierRand{} labellings), because ``same tier'' averages
the small--small end with the frontier--frontier end.

\noindent Three qualifications keep this modest. Nothing was assigned: developer
is an \emph{observed attribute} of judges we selected, confounded with training
data, alignment and serving path---the OpenAI cells are served over the vendor
CLI because the local proxy cannot pass the parameters those checkpoints
require, so the codebook is identical and the harness is not. The permutation is
therefore a valid test of whether the observed grouping is extreme among
regroupings of the judges we have, not a randomized experiment on developer. The
effect is also a between-group average, not a property of every pair:
\TierFrontCrossBeats{} of the twelve cross-developer frontier pairs agree better
than the weakest same-developer pair (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:frontier-pairs}). And two checkpoints from one developer share
training data and alignment, so their agreement bounds what \emph{correlated}
judges reach rather than what the task affords. What the design licenses is
this: upgrading the model does not fix adjudication, one model family does not
settle it, and a reported verdict rate is a property of its panel.

\paragraph{Is the schema the problem, and does it survive its own quotes?}
A natural objection is that the verdict fails because the instrument is
over-specified. The same three frontier judges coded the same frame twice more,
changing only the codebook---once with C1 and C4 alone, once with no coordinates
and the question asked in one step---all three arms scored on the same
\AblItems{} windows (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:codebook-ablation}). Verdict
$\kappa$ is \AblFiveK{} with five coordinates, \AblCFourK{} with two
(\AblCFourDelta{}, \AblCFourCI{}) and \AblDirectK{} with none
(\AblDirectDelta{}, \AblDirectCI{}). Simplification does not make adjudication
reproducible. What the arms expose is where the disagreement lives: across the
nine-judge pool the share of eligible windows a judge calls sound ranges from
\TierSurvivesLo\% to \TierSurvivesHi\% under one codebook on identical items.
The judges are not noisy about a shared threshold; they hold different
thresholds. \CensusQuoteRate\% of evidence quotes were located verbatim, and
restricting to the \QuoteVerifiedN{} windows (\QuoteVerifiedPct\%) where
\emph{every} judge's quote was verified moves verdict $\kappa$ only to
\QuoteVerdictK{}.

\section{What Exactly Breaks}
\label{sec:instrument}

The census cannot separate three explanations of its own negative result: the
adjudication task is ill-posed, the schema cannot be coded, or published
passages do not state what the schema asks for. Observational agreement cannot
separate them, because there is no reference. So we built one, and designed it
so that the third explanation is tested rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Design.}
Each of \InsTwoItems{} items is a short synthetic excerpt from an imaginary
paper: a setup passage describing how each side was evaluated, and a results
passage placing two numbers side by side. Behind it is a specification fixing,
for C1, C2, C4 and C5, whether the two sides agree and whether the excerpt
discloses the fact, from which the verdict follows by the rule of
\S\ref{sec:estimands}. Nothing is annotated. \emph{Disclosure} has three levels,
not two. In \textsc{full} every coordinate is stated. In
\textsc{withheld-neutral} a coordinate is withheld but the verdict is fixed by
something the excerpt does state---a disclosed unrepaired mismatch elsewhere, or
a matched operating point that repairs the withheld one---so all three verdict
classes occur equally and the constant baseline is \InsBalBase{}, identical to
\textsc{full}. In \textsc{withheld-decisive} the correct answer \emph{is} that
the passage does not say: a coordinate is withheld, every stated one agrees, and
nothing repairs it. That arm is degenerate on purpose---its reference never
varies, so its constant baseline is \InsForceBase{}---and it is scored against
that baseline and excluded from every pooled figure. This is the correction that
matters: in an earlier design \emph{every} withheld item was of the third kind,
so the withheld arm was single-class, its apparent accuracy sat below the
trivial predictor, and pooling it with a three-class arm manufactured the
baseline the headline was measured against. \emph{Presentation} is the second
factor, paired within specification: the same facts written once as one
dedicated sentence per coordinate and once as paper-style prose, reordered,
indirect and interleaved with sentences carrying no coordinate information.
\InsTwoJudges{} judges from three developers answer every item under the same
three codebooks used on the corpus.

\paragraph{The coordinates are codable, and the schema is not the problem.}
On passages that state them, judges recover the coordinate labels at
\InsFullCoord{} and agree on the verdict at $\kappa$ \InsFullK{}. The rule
composed with those labels reaches \InsTwoRule{} against a constant baseline of
\InsTwoBase{} on the non-degenerate items, the weakest judge included
(Table~\ref{tab:instrument-baselines-v2}). The rule cannot be \emph{wrong}
here---the reference is that rule applied to the item's specification---so what
this measures is coding accuracy carried through a deterministic function. C3 is
the exception that confirms \S\ref{sec:estimands}: judges recover it at chance
even when the passage states it. Writing the same facts as prose rather than as
a checklist costs something and not much: coordinate recovery falls from
\InsCanonCoord{} to \InsNatCoord{} and the composed rule from \InsCanonRule{} to
\InsNatRule{}, with inter-judge $\kappa$ unchanged (\InsCanonK{} against
\InsNatK{}).

\paragraph{What fails is silence, not disclosure as such.}
Withholding a coordinate is not by itself what breaks adjudication. When a
coordinate is withheld and something else still fixes the answer, judges lose
accuracy on the \emph{coordinate} (\InsFullCoord{} to \InsBalCoord{}) and
nothing on the verdict: the composed rule holds at \InsBalRule{} against
\InsFullRule{}, the judges' own verdict at \InsBalJudge{} against
\InsFullJudge{}, and agreement is if anything higher ($\kappa$ \InsBalK{}
against \InsFullK{}). The collapse is specific to the third arm, where the right
answer is that the passage does not say. There the judges' verdict accuracy is
\InsForceJudge{} and the composed rule \InsForceRule{}, against a constant
predictor scoring \InsForceBase{}, and agreement falls to $\kappa$
\InsForceK{}---from \InsFullK{} on the same schema, the same judges and prose of
the same kind. Judges do not read silence as \emph{not determinable}; they fill
it in, and differently from one another. That is the mechanism the census sees:
published passages state few coordinates, so nearly every real window sits in
the third regime, where a panel neither agrees nor is right, which is $\kappa$
\DecVerdictK{} in Table~\ref{tab:cross-paper-census}. It also bounds what a
checklist buys: disclosure does not make readers agree about a comparison that
was already decidable, it removes the case in which agreement is impossible.

\paragraph{Two coordinates or five?}
The fair comparison for a five-field checklist is against the best
two-coordinate \emph{estimator}, not against the five-coordinate rule with two
inputs deleted, and Table~\ref{tab:instrument-baselines-v2} reports every arm.
On the non-degenerate items the five-coordinate composed rule reaches
\InsFiveAcc{} and the strongest two-coordinate arm \InsBestTwoAcc{}, a paired
difference of \InsFiveVsTwoDiff{} with exact McNemar $p=\InsFiveVsTwoP$. The gap
widens where it matters: on the arm whose correct answer is that the passage
does not say, the five-coordinate instrument scores \InsForceFive{} and the
two-coordinate one \InsForceTwo{}, because asking about C2 and C5 is what makes
their absence visible. Against that stands the corpus, where the C1/C4 codebook
gives verdict $\kappa$ \AblCFourK{} and the five-coordinate one \AblFiveK{}
(\AblCFourDelta{}, \AblCFourCI{}). Both facts are real and they are about
different quantities---accuracy against a reference, and agreement without
one---so we claim only what holds in both. C1 and C4 are the minimum any
checklist can ask; C2 and C5 earn their place by making silence detectable and
because every security case in \S\ref{sec:cases} is unmatched on C2, not by
raising inter-judge agreement on papers as they are written today.

\section{What Follows for Practice}
\label{sec:practice}

\paragraph{Reviewers are not interchangeable, and they are the unit.}
Deciding comparability at scale means running a panel of model reviewers, and
that panel is not exchangeable: the nine-judge pool holds different thresholds
rather than differing in noise around one, with the share of eligible windows a
judge calls sound ranging from \TierSurvivesLo\% to \TierSurvivesHi\% on
identical items. A separate crossed
benchmark of 600 synthetic deployment decisions answered by \CMJmodels{}
reviewers from \CMJdevelopers{} developers reproduces this ($I^2$ \CMJCtwoIsq\%;
for \CMJSignBelowHalf\% of reviewer-by-stratum cells the \emph{sign} of an
instruction effect flips with a paraphrase; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-cmj}).
The consequence is a design one, and it is the general form of the
language-as-fixed-effect fallacy: if prompts are assigned to reviewers,
reviewers remain the treatment units after majority voting, and analysing at the
source inflates type-I error. The missing parameter is $\sigma_r$, and we
estimate it on two crossed designs of this task: on the constructed benchmark,
whose reference is fixed before any judge sees the item,
$\hat\sigma_r=\SigInsVal{}$ (reviewer bootstrap \SigInsCI{}, \SigInsRev{}
judges, \SigInsItems{} items); on the census pool, \SigCensusVal{}
(\SigCensusCI{}, \SigCensusRev{} judges, \SigCensusItems{} windows), which we
read only as a bracket because its reference is a leave-one-out majority of
judges who agree at $\kappa$ \DecVerdictK{}. Five public crossed annotation
corpora bracket these at \SigmaMin{}--\SigmaMax{} (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:sigma-r-anchors}). At $\sigma_r=0$ a source-level analysis is
correctly calibrated ($\SimAtZero\%$ at 80 sources); at the values we measure it
rejects a true null $\SimAtCensus\%$ to $\SimAtPanel\%$ of the time, and two
retrospectively executable reviewer-level analyses hold the nominal level
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sim}).

\paragraph{A pre-collection checklist.}
Four decisions follow (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:robust-rag-reporting-checklist}). \emph{State the evidence state
and the outcome unit} (C1, C4): these are the two fields whose absence makes
adjudication undecidable rather than merely hard. \emph{State the capability and
the action set} (C2, C5): they earn their place by making a silence
detectable---the five-coordinate instrument notices a withheld coordinate where
the two-coordinate one does not---and because every security case in
\S\ref{sec:cases} is unmatched on C2, not by raising inter-judge agreement.
\emph{Say whether a quoted number was transported or re-implemented}: it is the
one property an automated audit recovers against a reference. \emph{Draw the
unit map and power it}: at $\hat\sigma_r=\SigInsVal{}$ a twelve-reviewer
two-arm design has \SimTwelvePower\% power for the effect we simulate, so twelve
reviewers is a pilot, not a panel \citep{card2020power}; release per-reviewer
pre-adjudication votes so the analysis can be redone at the right unit.

\section{Conclusion}

In the frame this paper screens, cross-paper comparison in robust RAG is mostly
not what it appears to be: of the \ProvClassified{} candidates assigned a
provenance class, \ProvReimplemented{} re-implement the other system inside the
citing paper's own harness, and coding the windows the screen missed puts that
share at \RecUniverseShare\%. That distinction is what automation
recovers---$\kappa$ \DecProvK{} between judges, \PvAcc{} against a fixed
reference---and it locates the narrow stratum in which a quoted gap can be
undefined rather than imprecise. Adjudicating those gaps is not
recovered by any panel or codebook we can build, and the reason is narrower than
we previously claimed. Judges read stated coordinates at \InsFullCoord{} and
agree at $\kappa$ \InsFullK{}; withholding one costs them nothing while
something else still fixes the answer ($\kappa$ \InsBalK{}); and when the right
answer is that the passage does not say, they neither agree ($\kappa$
\InsForceK{}) nor answer correctly (\InsForceJudge{}, against a constant
predictor scoring \InsForceBase{}). Judges do not report silence, they resolve
it, and differently. That is the distance between $\kappa$ \InsFullK{} on
constructed prose and \DecVerdictK{} on published text, and why the coordinates
are a disclosure requirement, not a score.

\section*{Limitations}

The twelve worked comparisons are cases, not a census. Both sides of a case had
to be present in local full text with an explicit number, which favors
open-access and widely cited work, and the coordinate-matched re-reading exists
only where a paper reports an operating point at the other side's setting. Our
coding is ours, and the independent re-coding in \S\ref{sec:cases} reproduces it
on the binary distinction we use but on only \RecThreeWayPct\% of three-way
labels, which is why no numerical rate from the ledger appears anywhere in this
paper.

The census removes author selection but introduces its own limits. The frame is
a deterministic screen over open-access English full texts with a fixed
gazetteer and three declared strata, so it surfaces comparisons that fit those
shapes and misses those spread over a longer argument; the \FrameCandidates{}
candidates are a frame, not a sample of the literature, and every composition
figure in this paper is stated for that frame or for the re-weighted universe of
\RecUniverse{} windows, never for the field. The re-weighting rests on
\RecSampled{} coded windows from \RecPapers{} papers and on the assumption that
the same instrument behaves the same way inside and outside the frame. Its
judges are language models, so the agreement decomposition is a statement about
automated auditing. Because judges disagree on verdicts, no verdict-based rate
from the census appears anywhere in this paper; an earlier version reported a
higher failure rate for transported comparisons, and that contrast has been
removed rather than hedged, because a direction inferred from a label whose
$\kappa$ is \DecVerdictK{} is not weaker evidence than an estimate but the same
non-evidence. \CensusQuoteRate\% of evidence quotes were located verbatim, and
restricting to fully quote-verified items changes no conclusion.

The provenance reference is constructed, which bounds it in the same way the
comparability benchmark is bounded: it shows the deployed prompt and panel can
make the distinction on prose we wrote, and not that real windows elicit the
same error rates. The two facts that travel are that the label is not a
phrase-matching artefact---accuracy is \PvImplicit{} on items with no
declaration sentence---and that its errors do not favour either class, so the
measured share is not produced by a biased classifier. Its one systematic error
is over-commitment on genuinely undecidable passages, which inflates the
classified denominator rather than the re-implemented share.

The tier control identifies capability tier against developer, but not against
everything else. ``Frontier'' and ``small'' are the developers' own tiering, not
a measured capability, and the OpenAI cells are served over the vendor CLI
rather than the local proxy, because the proxy cannot pass the parameters those
checkpoints require---the same prompt and codebook, but not an identical
harness. The exact randomizations are valid at the judge level and they are
small: three developers give \TierDevRand{} partitions and six frontier
checkpoints give \TierPairRand{} matchings, so the smallest attainable
two-sided $p$ in the latter is $\TierPairP$. We report the developer contrast as
structure that survives a correct test, not as an effect size anyone should
reuse.

The constructed benchmark is templated prose with a synthetic specification, and
performance on it does not predict performance on natural paper text---which is
why it is reported beside the corpus results rather than instead of them. One
limit is structural and we state it plainly rather than leaving it to be found:
each item's reference verdict \emph{is} the decision rule applied to that item's
coordinate values, so the rule cannot be wrong on this benchmark by
construction. What the rule accuracy therefore measures is whether a judge can
read the coordinates off prose, propagated through a deterministic
function---not whether the rule is the right rule. Three things survive that
caveat. The coordinates are \emph{codable} from prose that states them, at
$\kappa$ \InsFullK{} against $\kappa$ \DecCoordKMax{} for the same judges on
published text, a factor of about four rather than the order of magnitude an
earlier version claimed. Writing the same facts as prose rather than as a
checklist costs a measurable but small amount (\InsCanonRule{} to
\InsNatRule{}), so readability is not the binding constraint. And the collapse
is specific to items whose correct answer is that the passage does not say,
which is the only arm on which agreement approaches what we observe on real
text. One thing we do \emph{not} claim from it: any field-level rate, since the
classes are balanced by construction. The five-versus-two comparison is reported
in both directions because the two settings disagree---five coordinates are more
accurate against this reference (\InsFiveAcc{} against \InsBestTwoAcc{},
McNemar $p=\InsFiveVsTwoP$) while two are no less reproducible on published text
(\AblCFourK{} against \AblFiveK{})---and neither result alone settles what a
checklist should ask for. The degenerate arm is degenerate on purpose and is
scored against its own constant baseline of \InsForceBase{}; it is evidence
about how judges treat silence, not about how codable the schema is.

The reviewer-heterogeneity anchors are crossed designs at scale, but two of the
three on-task ones are model panels, and the five public annotation corpora are
not comparability adjudication---they are offensiveness, toxicity, sentiment,
emotion and preference judgments, with each annotator scored against a
leave-one-out majority rather than an independent adjudication, which attenuates
individual accuracies and if anything understates $\sigma_r$. The census anchor
has the same defect in a sharper form and we no longer read it as an ability
estimate: its reference is a leave-one-out majority of judges who agree at
$\kappa$ \DecVerdictK{}, and \S\ref{sec:census} shows those judges hold
different \emph{thresholds}, so \SigCensusVal{} there re-expresses threshold
dispersion as much as ability variance. The constructed benchmark's
\SigInsVal{} \SigInsCI{}, whose reference is fixed before any judge sees the
item, is the one we use, and the type-I inflation is reported across the whole
anchored range rather than at a point. We draw only the weak conclusion the
anchors support: $\sigma_r=0$ is not a live possibility. The simulation uses
binary correctness, a common wrong-label state, balanced three-of-six panels and
a logit random-effects model; it demonstrates a design failure mode and the
analyses that repair it, not a prevalence, and the two remedies are less
efficient than a crossed mixed-effects model where reviewers rate disjoint item
sets. That the wrong-unit inflation exists is not new
\citep{clark1973language,judd2012stimuli}; what is new here is the parameter for
this task, and it is a model-panel parameter.

The crossed deployment benchmark is templated arithmetic prose with a synthetic
loss rule. Its model sample spans \CMJdevelopers{} developers but remains a
convenience sample; its capability effect's prediction interval and its
developer-level interval both contain zero and its utility effect is null, so we
draw only the reviewer-heterogeneity conclusion from it. The API-served models
were given a larger output-token budget than the open checkpoints and required a
decision normalisation, applied symmetrically to every reviewer, but the two
serving paths are not identical harnesses.

Every judgment in this paper is produced by a model or by a deterministic rule.
Nothing here establishes what a panel of people would do, and no claim should be
read as one. Local hashes authenticate bytes, not behavioral independence or
external preregistration.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This paper contains no human-subjects research. No annotation was collected from
any person, no participant was recruited or contracted, and no result reported
here derives from labels we gathered from people. Every judgment in the census,
the tier-control pool, the codebook ablation, the ledger re-coding, the two
constructed benchmarks, the out-of-frame recall sample and the deployment panel
is produced by a language model under a released prompt with released raw
outputs, or by a deterministic rule over released artifacts. We adopted this
design deliberately. A paper arguing that an unauditable declaration is not
evidence should not rest a section on one, and a fully machine-executed pipeline
is inspectable end to end by anyone who downloads it.

The published-paper audit uses public scholarly documents and introduces no
participant contact. The comparability cases quote short passages from published
papers for verification purposes only, and every such quotation is checked
verbatim against its cited line range by the released verifier. The five public
annotation corpora used as external reference points in
Table~\ref{tab:sigma-r-anchors} are re-analysed from their authors' public
releases under their stated licences; we contact no annotator, re-identify no
one, and report only aggregate variance components.

Two limits of this choice should be stated rather than glossed. Model judgments
are not human gold, so the agreement decomposition in \S\ref{sec:census} is a
statement about automated auditing and not about people, and the checklist in
\S\ref{sec:practice} is addressed to authors who will run such panels rather
than offered as a claim about human review. And the constructed benchmark
contains no participant data and no third-party quotation, so its realism is
bounded by our own templates. The reported open-model runs use deterministic
decoding and release raw outputs. No attack payloads or poisoned corpora are
released.

\section{Constructed Instrument Benchmark}
\label{sec:appendix-instrument}

\paragraph{Why the design was changed.}
An earlier version of this benchmark crossed disclosure with the reference
verdict in a way that made the withheld arm uninterpretable: because the
decision rule maps silence about a threatening coordinate to \emph{undefined},
every withheld item carried that verdict. On such an arm the constant predictor
scores $1.00$, so the judges' $0.91$ was \emph{below} the trivial baseline
rather than above it; an inter-judge $\kappa$ of $0.71$ there was largely a
measure of how often judges default to one label; and pooling that arm with a
three-class arm produced the $1/3$ constant baseline against which the headline
was reported. The design in \S\ref{sec:instrument} separates the three cases
that were confounded---a coordinate stated, a coordinate withheld where the
verdict is fixed by something else, and a coordinate withheld where the verdict
turns on the silence---keeps the verdict classes equinumerous inside each of the
first two, scores every arm against its own constant baseline, and reports the
third as the degenerate control it is.

\section{Assignment-Unit Simulation Details}
\label{sec:appendix-sim}

The simulation is a two-arm review study with \SimReviewers{} reviewers, six per
prompt, $G$ sources contributing six claims each, and a balanced three-of-six
panel per claim in both arms; baseline agreement is 75\% and source and claim
logit standard deviations are $.15$ and $.35$. Reviewer abilities are redrawn
from $N(0,\sigma_r^2)$ in every replicate, so the target is a
reviewer-population effect and a source-only analysis cannot be defended as
answering a realized-panel estimand instead; each cell uses 5{,}000 replicates
at $\alpha=.05$. \emph{Panel/src} is the status quo, a paired source $t$ test on
panel-majority correctness computable from released majority labels alone;
\emph{Votes/src} keeps the source-level standard error but uses individual
votes; \emph{Rev.\ $t$} tests the twelve reviewer accuracies and \emph{Rev.\ RI}
refers it to all $\binom{12}{6}=924$ arm assignments. The last two need only
pre-aggregation votes with reviewer identifiers. Adding sources makes the status
quo worse at every positive $\sigma_r$, because item precision improves while
the number of prompt assignments stays at twelve, and replacing majority labels
with individual votes does not help: the defect is the unit over which
uncertainty is computed, not the aggregation. Both reviewer-level analyses hold
the nominal level across the grid, with size between $3.7\%$ and $5.7\%$, and
their power is the binding constraint rather than their calibration.
