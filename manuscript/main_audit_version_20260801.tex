\title{Re-Implemented, Not Transported:\\
Auditing Cross-Paper Comparisons in Robust RAG}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Robust retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) papers share labels---noise
robustness, faithfulness, security, calibration---while answering different
decision questions, so a quoted cross-paper gap may not be a comparison at all.
We ask what an automated audit of such gaps can and cannot decide, and we
answer it by varying the evidence rather than only the model. A documented
screen over \FlowTexts{} full texts yields \FrameCandidates{} candidate
juxtapositions, coded independently by judges from three developers. One
judgment is recovered: whether a number was \emph{transported} from the other
paper or \emph{re-implemented} in this harness. Judges agree on it ($\kappa$
\DecProvK{}) and, scored on \PrivN{} \emph{real} windows against a reference set
by adjudicators that read both papers' full texts, the deployed panel reaches
\DepPvAcc{} \DepPvCI{} against a \DepPvBase{} baseline. In this frame the
distinction is lopsided: \ProvReimplemented{} of the \ProvClassified{}
candidates assigned a class re-implement the other system and
\ProvTransported{} transport a number. The adjudication is not recovered:
verdict $\kappa$ reaches \TierFrontierVerdictK{} for an all-frontier panel drawn
from a fully crossed pool of \TierPanelN{}, composing the decision rule from the
judges' own coordinate labels does not help (\RuleKDiff{} \RuleKDiffCI{}), and
neither does simplifying the schema. Three interventions then locate the cause
instead of assuming it. Giving the same judges the entire citing paper instead
of a \mbox{45-line} window raises verdict agreement by \LadVdDeltaCite{}
\LadVdDeltaCiteCI{}, so a large part of the census figure is an artefact of what
a screen can see---but it lands at \emph{fair}, not at reproducible. Giving them
an explicit \emph{the passage does not say} option, which they use on
\AbsShare\% of codings, moves it by \AbsKDiff{} \AbsKDiffCI{}, so the instrument
was not the obstacle. What closes the gap is neither: on constructed passages
that state their coordinates the same instrument reaches $\kappa$ \InsFullK{},
at \KShareIns\% of the ceiling its own marginals allow against \KShareCensus\%
on published text. Disclosure helps a reader who has the papers and does not
make the comparison reproducible, and the two facts together are what the
coordinates should be read as recommending. We release the documented frame, all
codings, four verifiers and every simulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a generator on retrieved evidence
\citep{lewis2020rag,dpr2020,realm2020}, and robust-RAG studies ask what happens
when that evidence is irrelevant, insufficient, conflicting, stale, weakly
sourced, or malicious. Two reported ``robustness'' gains may nevertheless
concern different retrieval snapshots, attacker capabilities, interventions,
outcome units, or deployment actions. Source verification is therefore
necessary but insufficient: a cross-paper claim must also establish that both
results answer a compatible decision question.

\paragraph{One case, in full.}
PoisonedRAG reports a ``97\% ASR by injecting 5 malicious texts for each target
question'' on Natural Questions, and its Table~4 places that beside four
baselines, the weakest---the Corpus Poisoning Attack of
\citet{poisoningRetrievalCorpora2023}---at $0.01$ \citep{poisonedRAG2024}. A
reader carries away that corpus poisoning does not work. Corpus poisoning's own
paper reports it placing an adversarial passage in the retrieved set for
$84.2\%$ of Natural Questions queries with a \emph{single} adversarial passage,
and $99.4\%$ with fifty.

The two settings share a corpus and a retriever and nothing else that matters,
and we state each fact where it lives. Both use Natural Questions and the
pre-trained Contriever; PoisonedRAG declares this as its default, retrieving
``5 most similar texts from the knowledge database as the context''
\citep[l.~681--684]{poisonedRAG2024}, while the $84.2$ and $99.4$ are the
Contriever row of the NQ block at $|\mathcal{A}|=1$ and $50$
\citep[l.~366--372]{poisoningRetrievalCorpora2023}. What differs is what is
counted: corpus poisoning's success is retrieval---``the percentage of queries
for which at least one adversarial passage is retrieved in the top-$k$
results'' \citep[l.~333--337]{poisoningRetrievalCorpora2023}, at top-$20$---while
PoisonedRAG's outcome is the fraction of target questions answered with the
attacker's chosen answer, which corpus poisoning never chooses. PoisonedRAG says
so: the three weakest baselines ``are ineffective because they cannot achieve
generation, generation, and retrieval condition, respectively''
\citep[l.~1338--1341]{poisonedRAG2024}. That is the whole content of the $0.01$,
and inside PoisonedRAG's harness it is the right number. A released verifier
re-opens every line range here and confirms each value and condition
(\MotivPassed{}/\MotivChecks{} checks pass).

The two figures are printed in different units, count different events, and
range over different retrieved sets, so the outcome coordinate is unmatched and
no common scope exists in either text. The comparison is undefined rather than
repairable: there is no operating point at which $1\%$ and $84.2\%$ answer one
question. What survives is that these two attacks cannot be ranked by these two
numbers---and that the paper being benchmarked said as much, in a sentence the
number outlived.

\paragraph{The question this paper answers.}
That case is instructive, but it is one case and it was assembled by hand. Two
things are unknown: how often the failure occurs, and whether finding it can be
automated so a reviewer could run the check. We build that screen and measure,
judgment by judgment, how much of it works. The answer splits sharply.
\emph{Reading} a comparison---whether a number was carried across from another
paper or produced again locally---is recovered, and validated on real windows
against a reference set by adjudicators that read both papers rather than a
window. \emph{Adjudicating} it is not recovered, by any panel we can build, any
simplification of the instrument, any amount of extra evidence, or an explicit
licence to abstain.

The last two are the point. An observational audit cannot distinguish a task
that published text underdetermines from judges that cannot report
underdetermination, so we stop observing and intervene, holding the judges, the
codebook and the items fixed while moving one thing at a time. Give the judges
the entire citing paper, then both papers, in place of the window: agreement
climbs, by \LadVdDeltaCite{} \LadVdDeltaCiteCI{}, and stops at \emph{fair}. Give
them a fourth verdict meaning \emph{the passage does not say}, worded
prominently and preferred under uncertainty: they use it freely and agreement
does not move. So part of the census failure is what a screen cannot see, none
of it is an instrument that cannot express doubt, and the remainder---the
distance to the $\kappa$ \InsFullK{} the same judges reach on passages written
to state their coordinates---is what only rewriting the papers would close.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A census of cross-paper juxtapositions in robust RAG with the sampling frame
documented rather than asserted: a flow from \FlowRetrieved{} retrieved records
to \FlowTexts{} extracted full texts to \FrameCandidates{} candidates
(Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:screening-frame-flow}), coded by independent judges
from three developers, establishing that \ProvReimplemented{} of the
\ProvClassified{} candidates assigned a provenance class re-implement the other
system rather than transporting its number, and that judges agree on this at
$\kappa$ \DecProvK{} while they do not agree on the verdict
(\S\ref{sec:census}).
(ii) A privileged-information reference on \PrivN{} \emph{real} frame windows,
adjudicated from both papers' full texts with mechanically located quotes,
replacing a constructed validation set on which every judge was perfect and
which therefore had no power to detect the bias it was invoked to rule out
(\S\ref{sec:census}).
(iii) Three interventions that test the paper's causal claim instead of
asserting it---an evidence ladder, an explicit-abstention arm and the
constructed benchmark---with composed-rule and disclosure analyses that rule out
rule execution and bound how much of the failure extra evidence repairs
(\S\ref{sec:census}, \S\ref{sec:instrument}).
(iv) A fully crossed tier-by-developer pool of \TierPanelN{} judges analysed at
the judge level, a three-arm codebook ablation, a source-verified ledger of
twelve worked comparisons, and a between-reviewer heterogeneity estimate over
\OwjNTotal{} judges---\OwjNOpen{} of them open-weight checkpoints run in a
single harness---reported as an interval rather than a design constant
(\S\ref{sec:cases}, \S\ref{sec:practice}).

\paragraph{Terms used throughout.}
A \emph{harness} is one paper's corpus snapshot, retriever, generator, metric
and evaluation code taken together; a number is \emph{transported} when it
crosses a harness boundary and \emph{re-implemented} when the citing paper
produced it inside its own. A \emph{window} is a fixed span of lines in one
extracted full text, the \emph{frame} is the set of windows a deterministic
screen returns, and a \emph{stratum} is a declared subset defined by a document
feature or a construction choice, never by an audit outcome. A
\emph{coordinate} is one of the five fields of \S\ref{sec:estimands}. We say
\emph{judge} for a model that codes a window or an item and \emph{reviewer} for
the abstract unit whose variability \S\ref{sec:practice} is about; every judge
here is a reviewer, and no person is. The frame, every judge's raw output, both
constructed benchmarks, three verifiers and every simulation are in the
supplementary material.

\section{Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

Architectures differ in where retrieval enters and how evidence is fused
\citep{drqa2017,knnlm2020,fid2021,retro2022,atlas2023,replug2024}, and
evaluation, trustworthiness, security, conflict and abstention surveys expose
different failure surfaces \citep{ragSurvey2024,ragEvaluationSurvey2024,
trustRAGCompass2024,securityRAGSurvey2026,xuKnowledgeConflicts2024,
wenAbstentionSurvey2025}. Common-harness comparisons improve empirical control
\citep{ragBestPractices2024,alushiRAGComparison2026} but do not resolve whether
a quoted cross-paper result concerns the same evidence state, capability
envelope, outcome or action. Our unit is an individual cross-paper
juxtaposition (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:main-prior-work-comparison}). Annotation
research separately distinguishes reproducibility from truth
\citep{artstein2008intercoder,dawid1979observer}, treats disagreement as signal
\citep{uma2021disagreement,plank2022labelvariation,davani2022disagreements},
and models rater and item effects
\citep{passonneau2014annotation,paun2018bayesian}; crossed designs require both
sources of variation \citep{baayen2008crossed}, clustering follows treatment
assignment \citep{abadie2023clustering}, and power belongs to the randomized
unit \citep{card2020power,vanmiltenburg2021preregistering}. Auditing how
evaluations are reported is likewise established
\citep{howcroft2020twenty,vanderlee2021human,belz2021systematic,
belz2023impossibility,clark2021human}, and we follow their reporting
conventions, including a PRISMA-style screening flow
\citep{page2021prisma}; we claim no prevalence those studies do not already
establish. That inference over the wrong factor inflates type-I error is also
not new: \citet{clark1973language} named the language-as-fixed-effect fallacy
and \citet{judd2012stimuli} gave the crossed random-effects remedy, and both
subsume the general form of our simulation, which we therefore report in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sim} rather than as a finding. What they do not
supply is the parameter, which has never been estimated for comparability
adjudication.

\section{What a Comparison Has to Fix}
\label{sec:estimands}

Four boundaries motivate the coordinates: evidence availability is not evidence
use \citep{irrelevantContext2023,raat2024,rgb2023,chainOfNote2023}; support,
authority and conflict are distinct, so a response can be correct but
unsupported or faithful to a superseded source
\citep{ragTruth2024,astuteRAG2025,freshllms2024}; natural retrieval failure and
adaptive attack are different games under different access, budget and
persistence assumptions \citep{poisonedRAG2024,phantom2024,certifiedRAG2024};
and a confidence signal is not an action policy
\citep{guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,selfRAG2023,crag2024,
sufficientContext2024}. Appendix~\ref{sec:robust-rag-landscape} develops these
with paper-level contrasts. Each side of a comparison is recorded on five
coordinates:

\noindent\textbf{C1} \emph{Evidence state}: what is observable at the decision
boundary---supported, distracting, insufficient, conflicting, stale, weakly
sourced or adversarially manipulated---with the corpus snapshot, retriever,
top-$k$ and whether a gold passage is present. \textbf{C2} \emph{Origin /
capability}: how that state arose---benign retrieval error, a constructed
stressor, drift, or an adversary---and for an adversary the access, persistence,
and crucially whether the budget counts documents \emph{injected into the
corpus} or \emph{retrieved into the context}, and whether the metric runs over
all queries or only triggered ones. \textbf{C3} \emph{Intervention}: which part
of the system changes. \textbf{C4} \emph{Outcome and unit}: what is measured and
in what unit---answer correctness, retrieval quality, claim support, attack
success, leakage, calibration, selective risk or cost, per question, response or
span. \textbf{C5} \emph{Policy / utility}: which response is selected under
which costs---answer, verify, re-retrieve, expose conflict, quarantine or
abstain---with the error, abstention and follow-up prices stated.

\noindent A claim-specific control check accompanies them (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:main-framework-overview}); these specialize distinctions from
evidence synthesis, selective prediction and security evaluation
\citep{richardson1995pico,chow1970reject,papernot2018sok}.

\paragraph{One decision rule, and why C3 is not in it.}
Record each coordinate on both sides from the sources alone, then apply, in
order. \textbf{(1)}~If C1, C2, C4 and C5 all match, the quoted gap stands
(\emph{survives}). \textbf{(2)}~Otherwise, if a side reports an operating point
at the other's setting, or the comparison holds on a strict subset, re-read
there and report the matched number (\emph{narrows}). \textbf{(3)}~Otherwise the
gap is \emph{undefined} and must be replaced by the common scope both studies
support, or dropped---never by a substitute number computed across an unmatched
coordinate.

C3 is deliberately absent from every branch. Call it the \emph{intervention
exclusion}: comparing two \emph{different systems} means, by construction, that
the intervention differs, so C3 is the thing being compared, not a condition on
comparability. A rule treating a C3 mismatch as threatening would declare
essentially the whole literature undefined, and the data bear this out twice.
C3 is labelled ``mismatch'' on \CThreeMismatchMaj\% of corpus items where the
panel reaches a majority---so near-constant that its $\kappa$ ceiling is
\KmaxCThree{} and the statistic carries no information---and even on
constructed passages that state it explicitly, judges agree at $\kappa$ $0.00$
(\S\ref{sec:instrument}). It is the only coordinate at chance in \emph{both}
settings. We record C3 because a reader needs to know what changed, and exclude
it from the decision because it always changed.

\section{Twelve Worked Comparisons}
\label{sec:cases}

We assembled comparisons of two kinds from \FlowTexts{} locally extracted full
texts: \emph{stated} ones an author made (\LedgerStated{}), and
\emph{survey-invited} juxtapositions a survey's grouping leads a reader to make
(\LedgerInvited{}), reported separately. A stated comparison may
\emph{transport} the other paper's number, \emph{re-implement} the other system
and print its own, or assert a relation while supplying \emph{no} number for the
other side. Every value is copied verbatim with a file and line range, and four
released checks run over the result: one re-opens each range and fails on any
numeral absent from the cited lines; one requires every quoted string this
manuscript prints to occur verbatim at a registered range; one checks that every
quantity we assert about our own artifacts equals the field that produced it;
and one re-verifies the motivating case of \S\ref{sec:introduction} condition by
condition. All \VerifChecks{} ledger and \MotivPassed{}/\MotivChecks{}
motivating-case checks pass. They establish that our quotations are faithful,
not that our \emph{judgments} are right---so \RecJudges{} judges from
\RecDevs{} developers re-coded every case from the neutral juxtaposition and the
excerpts alone, never seeing our labels (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:ledger-recoding}). The majority reproduces us on
\RecThreeWayPct\% of three-way labels ($\kappa$ \RecThreeWayK{}) and on
\RecBinaryPct\% of the binary distinction our conclusions use. We therefore
report no rate from the ledger, only what it supports: these comparisons, by
name, are not rankings. All five security cases involve an unmatched attacker
capability or objective; the audit does not flag everything, and CRAG's
$6.9$-point gap over Self-RAG at a shared backbone, retriever, dataset and
metric stands.

Two cases carry the argument and are developed in full in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-cases}: RobustRAG reporting ``reducing attack
success rates from over 90\% to approximately 10\%'' beside PoisonedRAG's
$0.97$, which
transports no number and whose certificate binds where PoisonedRAG reports
$0.48$; and a budget-matched re-reading that would turn PoisonedRAG's
$3.7$-point lead over Phantom into a $45.3$-point deficit, which we decline to
report because the two sides count different budgets, query populations and
objectives, leaving C4 unmatched with no common unit. By our own rule that
comparison is undefined, and an undefined comparison may not be replaced by a
signed substitute.

\section{What a Census Can and Cannot Decide}
\label{sec:census}

The worked ledger shows the failure exists but cannot say how common it is. So
we built the screen an author or reviewer would want---one that finds candidate
comparisons and adjudicates them---and measured how much of it works.

\paragraph{Where the corpus comes from, and how it was screened.}
The frame is the object of study, so its provenance is reported rather than
asserted (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:screening-frame-flow}). \FlowQueries{}
declared arXiv queries plus five venue and repository sources, at a fixed
snapshot (\FlowSnapshot{}), returned \FlowRetrieved{} records; \FlowOpenPDF{}
had an open PDF, \FlowDedup{} survived title-and-identifier de-duplication,
\FlowScreened{} were retained by a released relevance pass, and \FlowTexts{}
extracted to text with a line structure a window screen can index. Inclusion and
exclusion criteria, the de-duplication rule and the per-stage actor are
released. One edge is weak and the figure marks it weak: per-query hit counts
were not logged at the snapshot and live indices drift, so the first stage is
documented but not re-executable, while everything from the \FlowTexts{}
released texts downward is. Hence the scope limit we hold to throughout: this
frame supports composition statements about itself and about the re-weighted
window universe, and none about the robust-RAG literature.

A deterministic screen over those texts marks a window as a candidate when it
names a gazetteer system absent from that paper's own title and abstract region,
carries at least two values formatted as results, and matches one of three
declared strata: baseline-table language, explicit attribution of a number to
another paper, or a survey-style score table. Nothing in the screen is a
judgment; the strata are document features, never the audit outcome, and the
gazetteer, cues and number pattern are released. It returns \FrameCandidates{}
candidates in \FrameFiles{} papers, and this frame---not a selection from
it---is what we code. Three judges from three
developers---\texttt{gpt-5-mini}, \texttt{claude-opus-4.6} and
\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}---code every candidate independently from the
same passage plus $\pm45$ lines of context, the same codebook and a
seed-shuffled order, never seeing one another's output; \CensusCodedAll{}
returned a parseable coding from all three. Each returns a rationale first, then
eligibility, the two sides, a label per coordinate, a verdict, a matched number,
and a verbatim evidence quote we check mechanically. A second pass asks of every
eligible candidate whether the other paper's number was \emph{transported} here
or \emph{re-implemented} inside this harness. Labels combine by mechanical
majority. The codebook was revised once on a declared
\CensusDevSize{}-candidate development pass; \CensusDevCoded{} of those were
also coded by all three judges in the deployed pass and are removed, leaving the
\CensusHeldout{} held-out candidates used below.

\paragraph{Two judgments, one recovered.}
\CensusEligible{} candidates from \CensusPapers{} papers are eligible by
majority, and Table~\ref{tab:cross-paper-census} reports every field under
$AC_1$, Fleiss' $\kappa$, and the largest $\kappa$ those judges could have
reached without changing how often each uses each label
\citep[$\kappa_{\max}$;][]{umesh1989maxkappa,sim2005kappa}. The third column is
what makes the first two readable: $AC_1$ rewards prevalence, $\kappa$ punishes
it, and a low $\kappa$ is evidence of disagreement only where $\kappa$ had room
to be high. One judgment is reliable under all three: \emph{how the other number
was obtained} reaches $AC_1$ \DecProv{} and $\kappa$ \DecProvK{}. The
inferential judgment is reliable under none: \emph{does the comparison survive}
reaches $AC_1$ \DecVerdict{} and $\kappa$ \DecVerdictK{}, which is
\KShareVerdict\% of its own ceiling of \KmaxVerdict{}. The difference is
\DecGap{} \DecGapCI{} under $AC_1$ and \DecGapK{} \DecGapKCI{} under $\kappa$,
on the same items by the same judges, every interval resampling \emph{source
papers}. We do not claim the two judgments differ in \emph{kind} rather than in
difficulty---provenance is also the simpler question---only that one is
recoverable at this scale and the other is not. The same ceiling makes the
coordinate rows readable individually rather than uniformly: C3 alone is
uninterpretable, at \PrevCThree\% one label against a ceiling of \KmaxCThree{},
whereas C4 is \PrevCFour\% one label but has room (\KmaxCFour{}), so its
$\kappa$ of \DecCoordKMin{} is not a prevalence artefact (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:kappa-ceiling}).

\paragraph{Did the panel execute its own rule, and did it quote honestly?}
\S\ref{sec:estimands} states a rule over C1, C2, C4 and C5; the census reports
the verdict judges \emph{stated}. Composing the rule mechanically from each
judge's own coordinate labels, judges state what their coordinates imply on
\RuleFidelity{} of codings, and \RuleSurvivesContraN{} of \RuleSurvivesN{}
majority-\emph{survives} verdicts (\RuleSurvivesContra\%) are contradicted by
the panel's own coordinates. That is a real internal-validity defect, and it is
not an alternative explanation of the negative result: agreement on the
\emph{composed} verdict is $\kappa$ \RuleKComposed{} against \RuleKStated{}
stated, a difference of \RuleKDiff{} \RuleKDiffCI{}. Separately, every coding
must quote the passage it read, and \CensusQuoteRate\% of quotes are located
verbatim. Classifying all \QFNonEmpty{} non-empty quotes shows what the rest
are: \QFNormalised{} are verbatim after Unicode and de-hyphenation
normalisation, \QFElision{} abridge with an ellipsis whose fragments are
verbatim, \QFReserial{} reorder tokens present in the passage, which is what
reading a linearised two-column table produces, and only \QFAbsent{}
(\QFAbsentPct\%) are absent from the source document. So \QFSupported\% of
quotes are supported by the passage shown, and the fabrication rate of this
instrument is \QFAbsentPct\%, not \QFOtherPct\% (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:census-diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Is the provenance label right, and not merely agreed on?}
Agreement is not accuracy, so the label must be scored against a reference. The
submitted version scored it against constructed excerpts whose provenance was
fixed when each was written, and reported \PvAcc{}. That number should not have
been used for this purpose: across all six judges and all items, every
transported item was called transported and every re-implemented item
re-implemented, so the benchmark had \emph{zero} measured error on the two
classes the composition claim depends on, and the bias correction it licensed
used an identity confusion matrix and returned its input unchanged. We keep the
constructed result as what it is---evidence that the distinction is not a
phrase-matching artefact, since accuracy is \PvImplicit{} on items carrying no
declaration sentence---and replace the validation.

The replacement is a reference on \emph{real} windows. We draw \PrivN{} windows
from the \PrivPopulation{} the panel called eligible, stratified only on the
frame stratum and on whether the cited paper's full text is held locally---a
document feature and a corpus property, never a label or an outcome---giving
\PrivBoth{} windows for which both papers are available. Four adjudicators from
three developers then decide each window from the \emph{entire} citing paper
and, where available, the entire cited paper, and must support every answer with
a quote we locate mechanically in the document that adjudicator was shown; at
the both-texts tier, one quote in each paper. A reference label is established
where a majority of quote-verified adjudicators agree, and windows without one
are reported rather than dropped. This reference is not human gold and we make
no claim that it is; it is \emph{evidence-privileged}, better informed than the
instrument it scores by a documented margin, and auditable end to end.

Against it (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:privileged-reference}) the deployed panel
recovers provenance at \DepPvAcc{} \DepPvCI{} on \DepPvN{} windows, against a
majority-class baseline of \DepPvBase{}; recall is \RefRecallTrans{} on
transported windows and \RefRecallReimpl{} on re-implemented ones. The measured
confusion is not the identity---sensitivity \RefCorrSens{} and specificity
\RefCorrSpec{} for the transported class---and moves the re-implemented share of
the frame to \RefCorrShare\% against \RecFrameShare\% uncorrected. Removing the
adjudicator sharing a checkpoint with the deployed panel leaves accuracy at
\DepPvAccLoo{}. The same reference scores the verdict far less kindly, which is
the sharpest form of the paper's negative result: \DepVdAcc{} \DepVdCI{} against
a majority-class baseline of \DepVdBase{} on \DepVdN{} windows. Three frontier
judges reading a published passage recover the adjudication barely better than a
constant predictor. The reference is also harder to establish here---a
quote-verified majority exists on \RefVdN{} of \PrivN{} windows against
\RefPvN{}---so the disagreement is not confined to weak judges reading short
passages.

\paragraph{What the frame is made of, and does that survive the screen?}
Of the \ProvCoded{} eligible candidates all three judges labelled,
\ProvClassified{} are assigned a provenance class; \ProvReimplemented{} of those
are re-implementations---the citing paper re-ran the other system in its own
corpus, retriever, generator and metric---and \ProvTransported{} transport a
number across a harness boundary. Inside this frame, cross-paper comparison in
robust RAG is therefore mostly a within-harness activity, which is this paper's
most useful positive fact: the incommensurability of \S\ref{sec:cases} has a
narrow, identifiable habitat that an automated screen can find even though it
cannot adjudicate what is inside it. We report no verdict-based failure rate,
because a rate computed from a label whose $\kappa$ is \DecVerdictK{} is the
same non-evidence as an estimate.

That composition might be an artefact of a screen whose first stratum is
baseline-table language, since baseline tables are where re-implementations
live. Against a universe of \RecUniverse{} windows naming another paper's system
and carrying a result-formatted value, \RecSampled{} of the \RecMissed{} the
screen missed were coded by the same judges under the same prompts (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:screen-recall}). Missed windows are eligible far less often
(\RecEligMissed{} against \RecEligFrame{}), and recall is \RecTrans{}
\RecTransCI{} for transported juxtapositions against \RecReimpl{}
\RecReimplCI{} for re-implemented ones, so the screen surfaces a transported
juxtaposition more readily, not less. Re-weighting moves the re-implemented
share \emph{up}, from \RecFrameShare\% to \RecUniverseShare\%
\RecUniverseShareCI{}. That second figure turns on \RecTransEligN{} sampled
windows, and we say so where we use it: reassigning all of them moves it to
\RecPerturbAll\%, doubling their count to \RecPerturbDouble\%, and a
paper-clustered bootstrap gives \RecPaperCI{}. The direction survives every
perturbation the data admit and the magnitude does not, so we report both
figures and claim neither for the literature.

\section{What Exactly Breaks}
\label{sec:instrument}

The census cannot separate three explanations of its own negative result: the
adjudication task is underdetermined by published passages, the schema cannot be
coded, or the judges cannot report underdetermination even when they detect it.
Observational agreement cannot separate them. So we intervene three times,
holding the judges, the codebook and the items fixed and moving one thing at a
time: the evidence available, the answer set available, and the passage itself.
The three answers are different, and together they say something narrower and
better supported than the submitted version claimed.

\paragraph{Intervention 1: give the judges the whole paper.}
If the census fails because a \mbox{45-line} window does not disclose the
coordinates, then disclosing them should repair it, and the strongest available
disclosure is the papers themselves. The same three judges re-code \PrivN{}
sampled windows under the same codebook, once from the entire citing paper and
once---on the \PrivBoth{} windows where we hold it---from both papers in full
(Table~\ref{tab:interventions}). That is roughly a hundredfold increase in
evidence, including the setup sections that state how baseline numbers were
obtained.

It helps, measurably, and it is not enough. Verdict agreement rises from
$\kappa$ \LadVdWinK{} at the window to \LadVdCiteK{} with the citing paper in
full, a paired difference of \LadVdDeltaCite{} \LadVdDeltaCiteCI{} resampling
source papers; adding the cited paper gives \LadVdBothK{}, within noise of the
citing paper alone on the windows both cover. So the census figure of
\DecVerdictK{} is in large part an artefact of what a screen can see, and this
is the first interventional evidence here that disclosure would help a real
reader. But a fivefold rise lands at \emph{fair}, not reproducible: the same
instrument reaches $\kappa$ \InsFullK{} on constructed passages that state their
coordinates, and no amount of the actual papers closes that distance.

The provenance arm is the positive control that makes this reading defensible.
The identical intervention takes provenance from $\kappa$ \LadPvWinK{} to
\LadPvCiteK{} and \LadPvBothK{} (\LadPvDeltaBoth{} \LadPvDeltaBothCI{}), which
is near-perfect, because whether a number was transported \emph{is} settled by
comparing the two documents. The ladder does what a manipulation should: it
saturates the judgment whose answer the added evidence contains, and moves but
does not resolve the judgment whose answer it does not.

\paragraph{Intervention 2: let the judges say the passage does not say.}
The remaining internal explanation is the instrument. The deployed codebook
offers \emph{survives}, \emph{narrows} and \emph{undefined}, and
\emph{undefined} means a disclosed, unrepaired mismatch---so a judge detecting
silence has nowhere to put it, and this paper proves that mechanism on a
neighbouring label, where a fourth provenance option moved the
asserted-no-number class from $0\%$ to full recovery. We therefore add a fourth
verdict, \texttt{not\_determinable\_from\_text}, described in the same register
as the other three and preferred under uncertainty, and change nothing else.

Judges use it, on \AbsShare\% of codings and by every judge (\AbsPerJudgeLo\% to
\AbsPerJudgeHi\%), and their \emph{survives} rate falls by \AbsSurvivesDrop{},
so the deployed instrument was indeed suppressing a report of silence.
Agreement still does not follow. Collapsing the new label back onto
\emph{undefined}, so both arms live in one three-label space and a change is a
change in how items are partitioned rather than a relabelling, verdict $\kappa$
moves by \AbsKDiff{} \AbsKDiffCI{}. The judges now agree that many passages do
not say; they do not agree about \emph{which}.

\paragraph{Does any of this depend on the judges being proprietary?}
Every judge above is an API-served checkpoint, so the negative result could be a
property of one class of model, and $\sigma_r$ below would be estimated from a
narrow capability band. We therefore add \OwjNOpen{} open-weight checkpoints
answering byte-identical prompts under greedy decoding in one harness, their
weights and revisions pinned. The census result replicates and if anything
strengthens: on the \OwjCensusN{} windows all pools cover, verdict $\kappa$ is
\OwjKApi{} for the API pool and \OwjKOpen{} for the open pool, against a ceiling
of \OwjKmaxOpen{} their own marginals would have permitted (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:open-weight-pool}). The abstention
result replicates too: open judges take the abstention option on
\OwjAbsShare\% of codings and collapsing it moves $\kappa$ by \OwjAbsDiff{}.
Adjudication is not recovered by open models either, and not because they label
too uniformly to agree.


\paragraph{Intervention 3: write passages that do state the coordinates.}
The third intervention is the constructed benchmark, the only one that moves the
verdict decisively. Each of \InsTwoItems{} items is a short synthetic excerpt---a
setup passage describing how each side was evaluated, and a results passage
placing two numbers side by side---behind which a specification fixes, for C1,
C2, C4 and C5, whether the two sides agree and whether the excerpt discloses the
fact, from which the verdict follows by the rule of \S\ref{sec:estimands}.
\emph{Disclosure} has three levels: \textsc{full} states every coordinate;
\textsc{withheld-neutral} withholds one but fixes the verdict by something the
excerpt does state, so all three verdict classes occur equally at a constant
baseline of \InsBalBase{}; and \textsc{withheld-decisive} makes the correct
answer \emph{that the passage does not say}, a degenerate arm whose constant
baseline is \InsForceBase{} and which is scored against that and excluded from
every pooled figure. \emph{Presentation} is a second, paired factor: the same
facts as one sentence per coordinate, and as paper-style prose.
\InsTwoJudges{} judges from three developers answer every item.

On passages that state them, judges recover the coordinates at \InsFullCoord{}
and agree on the verdict at $\kappa$ \InsFullK{}; the rule composed with those
labels reaches \InsTwoRule{} against a constant baseline of \InsTwoBase{} on the
non-degenerate items, the weakest judge included
(Table~\ref{tab:instrument-baselines-v2}). C3 is the exception that confirms
\S\ref{sec:estimands}: judges recover it at chance even when it is stated.
Writing the same facts as prose costs a measurable but small amount---coordinate
recovery \InsCanonCoord{} to \InsNatCoord{}, composed rule \InsCanonRule{} to
\InsNatRule{}, inter-judge $\kappa$ unchanged---so readability is not the
binding constraint.

\paragraph{What fails is silence, and how much of it evidence can repair.}
Withholding a coordinate is not by itself what breaks adjudication. When one is
withheld and something else still fixes the answer, judges lose accuracy on the
\emph{coordinate} (\InsFullCoord{} to \InsBalCoord{}) and nothing on the
verdict, and agreement is if anything higher ($\kappa$ \InsBalK{} against
\InsFullK{}). The collapse is specific to the arm where the right answer is that
the passage does not say: verdict accuracy \InsForceJudge{} and composed rule
\InsForceRule{} against a constant predictor scoring \InsForceBase{}, with
agreement at $\kappa$ \InsForceK{}.

The four interventions therefore place the census result between two bounds.
About a fifth of a $\kappa$ point is recoverable by supplying evidence the
window omitted (\LadVdDeltaCite{} \LadVdDeltaCiteCI{}). None is recoverable by
giving the judge somewhere to put a report of silence (\AbsKDiff{}
\AbsKDiffCI{}), by running the rule for it (\RuleKDiff{} \RuleKDiffCI{}), or by
changing the model class (open-weight $\kappa$ \OwjKOpen{}). What remains---the
distance from \LadVdCiteK{} to \InsFullK{}---is what only rewriting the papers
would close. The honest summary is neither that judges cannot report silence nor
that evidence is irrelevant, both of which we tested and disproved: it is that
these passages, even read in full, do not fix the comparison.

\paragraph{Two coordinates or five?}
The fair comparison for a five-field checklist is against the best
two-coordinate \emph{estimator} (Table~\ref{tab:instrument-baselines-v2}). On
the non-degenerate items the five-coordinate composed rule reaches
\InsFiveAcc{} against \InsBestTwoAcc{}, a paired difference of
\InsFiveVsTwoDiff{} with exact McNemar $p=\InsFiveVsTwoP$, and the gap widens on
the arm whose correct answer is that the passage does not say (\InsForceFive{}
against \InsForceTwo{}), because asking about C2 and C5 is what makes their
absence visible. Against that stands the corpus, where the C1/C4 codebook gives
verdict $\kappa$ \AblCFourK{} and the five-coordinate one \AblFiveK{}
(\AblCFourDelta{}, \AblCFourCI{}). The two settings measure different
quantities---accuracy against a reference, and agreement without one---so we
claim only what holds in both: C1 and C4 are the minimum any checklist can ask,
and C2 and C5 earn their place by making silence detectable, not by raising
inter-judge agreement on papers as they are written today.

\section{What Follows for Practice}
\label{sec:practice}

\paragraph{Reviewers are not interchangeable, and they are the unit.}
Deciding comparability at scale means running a panel of model reviewers, and
that panel is not exchangeable: the nine-judge pool holds different thresholds
rather than differing in noise around one, with the share of eligible windows a
judge calls sound ranging from \TierSurvivesLo\% to \TierSurvivesHi\% on
identical items. The open-weight pool makes the point sharply, because capacity is
measurable there: across \OwjNOpen{} checkpoints spanning \OwjPairSize{} to
\OwjBigSize{} the same share runs from \OwjSurvLo\% to \OwjSurvHi\%, the two
extremes sit within one parameter decade of each other, the largest checkpoint
lands mid-range at \OwjBigSurv\%, and two \OwjPairSize{} checkpoints---
\texttt{\OwjPairLoName{}} and \texttt{\OwjPairHiName{}}---read identical
windows at \OwjPairLo\% and \OwjPairHi\%. Threshold is not a function of
scale.
That is also why the verdict's $\kappa$ ceiling is only \KmaxVerdict{}. A separate crossed benchmark of 600 synthetic deployment
decisions answered by \CMJmodels{} reviewers from \CMJdevelopers{} developers
reproduces the heterogeneity ($I^2$ \CMJCtwoIsq\%;
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-cmj}). The consequence is the general form of the
language-as-fixed-effect fallacy: if prompts are assigned to reviewers,
reviewers remain the treatment units after majority voting, and analysing at the
source inflates type-I error. The missing parameter is $\sigma_r$, and the objection to the submitted estimate was that six judges cannot determine it: its interval spanned a factor of \SigInsRatio{}. The fix is more reviewers, so we estimate it on \OwjNTotal{} judges---\OwjNApi{} API-served and \OwjNOpen{} open-weight---all answering the constructed benchmark, whose reference verdict is fixed before any judge sees the item. That gives $\hat\sigma_r=\OwjSigPooled{}$ \OwjSigPooledCI{}, an interval spanning a factor of \OwjSigRatio{} rather than \SigInsRatio{}. It also shows the narrow pool was biased downward, not merely imprecise: the API judges alone give \OwjSigApi{} because they occupy a narrow capability band, and adding open checkpoints that reach lower accuracies raises it. Five public crossed annotation corpora sit inside the same envelope at \SigmaMin{}--\SigmaMax{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:sigma-r-anchors}). We draw only the conclusion holding everywhere
in it: at $\sigma_r=0$ a source-level analysis is calibrated
($\SimAtZero\%$ at 80 sources), and at every value the anchors admit it rejects
a true null \SigTypeILo\% to \SigTypeIHi\% of the time, while two
retrospectively executable reviewer-level analyses hold the nominal level
throughout (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-sim}).

\paragraph{A pre-collection checklist.}
Four decisions follow (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:robust-rag-reporting-checklist}), and we state what supports each.
\emph{State the evidence state and the outcome unit} (C1, C4): their absence is
what makes adjudication undecidable rather than merely hard. The support is
interventional---supplying the papers in full raises verdict agreement by
\LadVdDeltaCite{} \LadVdDeltaCiteCI{}---not observational, since windows that
merely \emph{mention} more coordinate terms do not yield more reproducible
verdicts (\DiscDiff{} \DiscDiffCI{}). Stating a coordinate means stating both
sides' values for it, not naming it. \emph{State the capability and the action
set} (C2, C5): they make a silence detectable, and every security case in
\S\ref{sec:cases} is unmatched on C2. \emph{Say whether a quoted number was
transported or re-implemented}: it is the one property an automated audit
recovers against a reference on real windows (\DepPvAcc{} \DepPvCI{}), and the
one the evidence ladder drives to near-perfect agreement. \emph{Draw the unit
map and power it}: release per-reviewer pre-adjudication votes so the analysis
can be redone at the right unit, and treat a twelve-reviewer two-arm design as a
pilot \citep{card2020power}.

\noindent What we do \emph{not} recommend is deploying this audit as an
adjudicator. Against the privileged reference the deployed panel's verdict
accuracy is \DepVdAcc{} against a majority-class baseline of \DepVdBase{}. What
it recovers is the comparison and its provenance, and that is what an automated
screen should hand a reviewer.

\section{Conclusion}

In the frame this paper documents and screens, cross-paper comparison in robust
RAG is mostly not what it appears to be: of the \ProvClassified{} candidates
assigned a provenance class, \ProvReimplemented{} re-implement the other system
inside the citing paper's own harness. That distinction is what automation
recovers---$\kappa$ \DecProvK{} between judges, \DepPvAcc{} \DepPvCI{} against a
reference built by reading both papers in full---and it locates the narrow
stratum in which a quoted gap can be undefined rather than imprecise.

Adjudicating those gaps is not recovered, and four interventions decompose why.
Supplying the entire citing paper raises verdict agreement by \LadVdDeltaCite{}
\LadVdDeltaCiteCI{}, so a large share of the census figure is an artefact of
what a \mbox{45-line} screen can see. An explicit \emph{the passage does not
say} option, taken on \AbsShare\% of codings, moves it by \AbsKDiff{}
\AbsKDiffCI{}; composing the rule from the judges' own coordinates moves it by
\RuleKDiff{} \RuleKDiffCI{}; and \OwjNOpen{} open-weight checkpoints reproduce
the failure at $\kappa$ \OwjKOpen{}. Neither the option set, nor rule execution,
nor the model class is the obstacle. What is left is the distance from
\LadVdCiteK{}, with both papers read end to end, to the $\kappa$ \InsFullK{} the
same instrument reaches on prose written to state its coordinates. That distance
is a fact about how the field writes rather than about how models read, and it
is why the coordinates belong in a disclosure requirement rather than in a
score---and why what a screen should hand a reviewer is the comparison and its
provenance, not the verdict.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{What this paper supersedes.}
Three results in an earlier version of this work do not survive and are replaced
rather than hedged, and we group the accounting here instead of narrating it
through the argument. A verdict-based failure rate for transported comparisons
has been removed, because a direction inferred from a label whose $\kappa$ is
\DecVerdictK{} is not weaker evidence than an estimate but the same
non-evidence. A constructed provenance benchmark that reported \PvAcc{} has been
demoted from validation to a lexical control, because every judge was perfect on
the two classes the composition claim depends on, so it had no power to detect
the bias it was invoked to rule out and the correction it licensed was the
identity map; \S\ref{sec:census} replaces it with a reference on real windows.
And an earlier disclosure benchmark scored a single-class arm against a
three-class baseline; \S\ref{sec:instrument} separates the three cases it
confounded and scores every arm against its own constant predictor.

\paragraph{The frame.}
The twelve worked comparisons are cases, not a census; both sides had to be
present in local full text with an explicit number, which favors open-access and
widely cited work. Our coding is ours, and the independent re-coding in
\S\ref{sec:cases} reproduces it on the binary distinction we use but on only
\RecThreeWayPct\% of three-way labels, which is why no numerical rate from the
ledger appears in this paper. The census removes author selection but introduces
its own limits. Its first stage is documented and not re-executable: per-query
hit counts were not logged at the snapshot date, so a third party can reproduce
the frame from the \FlowTexts{} released texts downward but cannot reconstruct
the population those texts were drawn from. Every composition figure is stated
for the frame or for the re-weighted universe of \RecUniverse{} windows, never
for the field, and the re-weighted figure turns on \RecTransEligN{} sampled
windows, which is why \S\ref{sec:census} prints that count beside it and reports
the perturbation range \RecPerturbDouble\%--\RecPerturbAll\%.

\paragraph{The reference.}
The privileged reference is not human gold and is not a substitute for one. Its
adjudicators are language models; what makes it a reference is that they read
both papers where the instrument read \mbox{45 lines}, and that every vote
counted toward it carries a mechanically located quote. Two of its four
adjudicators share a developer and one shares a checkpoint with the deployed
panel, because those are the strongest checkpoints those developers serve, so
independence is by evidence and by verification rather than by model identity;
the leave-that-judge-out column exists for this reason. It is established on
\RefPvN{} of \PrivN{} windows for provenance and \RefVdN{} for the verdict, and
windows without a quote-verified majority are reported, not dropped. Nothing here
establishes what a panel of people would do.

\paragraph{The interventions.}
The evidence ladder shows that supplying the full papers raises verdict
agreement without making it reproducible. That is evidence about how much of the
census figure is an evidence deficit and how much is not; it is not proof that
the residue is underdetermination, since a judge may fail to use evidence it is
given, and \PrivN{} windows from \PrivPapers{} papers is a small design. We
report the provenance arm of the same ladder as the positive control that makes
the reading defensible, since the identical intervention drives that judgment to
near-perfect agreement. The abstention arm changes two things at once by
necessity, an option and an instruction to prefer it, so it identifies their
joint effect. Neither is a randomized experiment on disclosure, which cannot be
run on published text; the constructed benchmark is the closest available
substitute and is synthetic by construction.

Two of the ladder's own numbers should temper it. Its both-texts tier covers
only \PrivBoth{} windows, so its interval is wide and it is not distinguishable
from the citing-paper tier. And even the privileged adjudicators fail to reach a
quote-verified majority on the verdict for \RefVdNoMaj{} of \PrivN{} windows,
against \RefPvNoMaj{} for provenance: the disagreement this paper measures is
not confined to weak judges reading short passages.

\paragraph{The instruments.}
The tier control identifies capability tier against developer but not against
everything else: ``frontier'' and ``small'' are the developers' own tiering, and
the OpenAI cells are served over the vendor CLI rather than the local proxy,
because the proxy injects a parameter those checkpoints reject---the same prompt
and codebook, but not an identical harness. The exact randomizations are valid
at the judge level and they are small. The constructed benchmark is templated
prose with a synthetic specification, and one limit is structural: each item's
reference verdict \emph{is} the decision rule applied to that item's coordinate
values, so the rule cannot be wrong on it by construction. What its accuracy
measures is whether a judge can read coordinates off prose, propagated through a
deterministic function. We claim no field-level rate from it, since its classes
are balanced by construction. The five-versus-two comparison is reported in both
directions because the two settings disagree, and neither result alone settles
what a checklist should ask for.

\paragraph{The parameter.}
The reviewer-heterogeneity estimate now rests on \OwjNTotal{} judges rather
than six, which narrows its interval to a factor of \OwjSigRatio{}, but they are
all model judges and the five public annotation corpora are not comparability
adjudication. The census anchor's reference is a leave-one-out majority of
judges who agree at $\kappa$ \DecVerdictK{}, so it re-expresses threshold
dispersion as much as ability variance; the constructed benchmark's reference is
fixed before any judge sees the item and is the one we use. Which checkpoints
enter the pool still moves the estimate---\OwjSigApi{} for the API judges alone
against \OwjSigPooled{} pooled---so $\sigma_r$ is a property of a panel a
designer assembles, not a constant of the task, and we report the envelope
\SigEnvLo{}--\SigEnvHi{} with only the conclusion that holds across it.
That the wrong-unit inflation exists is not new
\citep{clark1973language,judd2012stimuli}; what is new is the parameter for this
task, and it is a model-panel parameter with a wide interval. The simulation
uses binary correctness, a common wrong-label state, balanced three-of-six
panels and a logit random-effects model; it demonstrates a design failure mode
and the analyses that repair it, not a prevalence.

The crossed deployment benchmark is templated arithmetic prose with a synthetic
loss rule; its model sample spans \CMJdevelopers{} developers but remains a
convenience sample, and its capability and utility effects are null, so we draw
only the reviewer-heterogeneity conclusion from it. Every judgment in this paper
is produced by a model or by a deterministic rule. Local hashes authenticate
bytes, not behavioral independence or external preregistration.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This paper contains no human-subjects research. No annotation was collected from
any person, no participant was recruited or contracted, and no result reported
here derives from labels we gathered from people. Every judgment in the census,
the tier-control pool, the codebook ablation, the ledger re-coding, the
privileged-information reference, the evidence ladder, the abstention arm, the
two constructed benchmarks, the out-of-frame recall sample and the deployment
panel is produced by a language model under a released prompt with released raw
outputs, or by a deterministic rule over released artifacts. We adopted this
design deliberately. A paper arguing that an unauditable declaration is not
evidence should not rest a section on one, and a fully machine-executed pipeline
is inspectable end to end by anyone who downloads it.

This choice has a cost we state rather than gloss. The privileged reference in
\S\ref{sec:census} is a reference by virtue of evidence and mechanical
verification, not by virtue of being human, and readers should not substitute it
for a human standard; where the manuscript says a judgment is ``recovered'' it
means recovered against that reference and against inter-judge agreement, and
nothing more. Model judgments are not human gold, so the agreement decomposition
in \S\ref{sec:census} is a statement about automated auditing and not about
people, and the checklist in \S\ref{sec:practice} is addressed to authors who
will run such panels rather than offered as a claim about human review.

The published-paper audit uses public scholarly documents and introduces no
participant contact. The comparability cases quote short passages from published
papers for verification purposes only, and every such quotation is checked
verbatim against its cited line range by the released verifier. The five public
annotation corpora used as external reference points in
Table~\ref{tab:sigma-r-anchors} are re-analysed from their authors' public
releases under their stated licences; we contact no annotator, re-identify no
one, and report only aggregate variance components.

Two limits of this choice should be stated rather than glossed. The constructed
benchmark contains no participant data and no third-party quotation, so its
realism is bounded by our own templates; and the privileged reference quotes
short passages from published papers for verification purposes only, every such
quotation being checked verbatim against its cited line range by the released
verifier. The reported open-model runs use deterministic decoding and release
raw outputs. No attack payloads or poisoned corpora are released.

\section{Assignment-Unit Simulation Details}
\label{sec:appendix-sim}

The simulation is a two-arm review study with \SimReviewers{} reviewers, six per
prompt, $G$ sources contributing six claims each, and a balanced three-of-six
panel per claim in both arms; baseline agreement is 75\% and source and claim
logit standard deviations are $.15$ and $.35$. Reviewer abilities are redrawn
from $N(0,\sigma_r^2)$ in every replicate, so the target is a
reviewer-population effect and a source-only analysis cannot be defended as
answering a realized-panel estimand instead; each cell uses 5{,}000 replicates
at $\alpha=.05$. \emph{Panel/src} is the status quo, a paired source $t$ test on
panel-majority correctness computable from released majority labels alone;
\emph{Votes/src} keeps the source-level standard error but uses individual
votes; \emph{Rev.\ $t$} tests the twelve reviewer accuracies and \emph{Rev.\ RI}
refers it to all $\binom{12}{6}=924$ arm assignments. The last two need only
pre-aggregation votes with reviewer identifiers. Adding sources makes the status
quo worse at every positive $\sigma_r$, because item precision improves while
the number of prompt assignments stays at twelve, and replacing majority labels
with individual votes does not help: the defect is the unit over which
uncertainty is computed, not the aggregation. Both reviewer-level analyses hold
the nominal level across the grid, with size between $3.7\%$ and $5.7\%$, and
their power is the binding constraint rather than their calibration.
