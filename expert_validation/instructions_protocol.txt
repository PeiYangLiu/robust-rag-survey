Protocol-condition instructions

You will judge whether pairs of robust-RAG papers can be directly compared.
Apply the five-coordinate comparability protocol below.

Coordinates
-----------
F: failure type
   relevance/noise; sufficiency; consistency/conflict; provenance/freshness;
   integrity/adversarial corruption; uncertainty/OOD.

A: evidence assumption
   benign retrieval error; benchmark-constructed stressor; naturally occurring
   conflict; stale or low-quality source; attacker-created evidence; privacy or
   extraction threat. For security papers, record corpus write access, query
   control, retriever visibility, generator access, insertion budget, and
   objective when reported.

I: intervention point
   corpus/index; retriever; reranker; context assembly/compression; generator
   prompting or training; verifier; system-control policy; evaluation artifact.

M: metric target
   answer correctness; retrieval recall; evidence support; citation faithfulness;
   attack success; calibration error; selective risk/coverage; cost/latency;
   human preference.

Act: action policy
   answer; abstain; re-retrieve; filter; verify; expose conflict; ask
   clarification; escalate; quarantine; recover; unreported.

Verdict rules
-------------
- valid: all five coordinates align. The action policy must be explicitly stated
  or evaluated; it cannot be inferred from a QA setup.
- partial: the failure type is shared, but the intervention point, metric target,
  or action policy differs, so the papers should be discussed as complementary
  components rather than substitutes.
- invalid: the evidence assumption or objective defines a different game, such as
  natural conflict versus attacker-created corruption or answer correctness
  versus citation support as the sole target.
- under-specified: at least one coordinate needed for comparison is unobtainable
  from the paper description. Missing action policy caps the verdict at
  under-specified.

For each paper pair, record the five coordinates when possible, the verdict, the
breaking coordinate(s), confidence from 1 to 5, decision time in seconds, and a
short rationale.
